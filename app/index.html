The problem

About 6 crore people over 70 are eligible. Fewer than 1 in 4 have the card.

The government’s health card for people over 70 doesn’t depend on income. Part of what stands in the way is the application form itself. For anyone with an unsteady hand or tired eyes, it has:

  • Captchas of six bent, crowded letters to copy, on more than one screen.
  • OTPs sent to the phone linked to Aadhaar, which is often a son’s or daughter’s, sometimes in another city.
  • Sessions that expire if the page sits idle while someone fetches that code, and clear everything typed so far.
  • Small text and small boxes, and state, district and ward lists that open only one after another.

On the portal, who does what

The helper does, after a yes

  • Shows the step in large text and reads it aloud
  • Chooses state, district and ward from the lists
  • Presses Next, Continue and Proceed
  • Says who to ask for the OTP: “Ask Rahul for the code sent to the phone ending 4321”

Your parent does

  • Types the Aadhaar number
  • Reads and types the captcha
  • Types the OTP
  • Ticks the consent box
  • Presses the final Submit

Limits

What it will not do

  • It never solves a captcha. It doesn’t read or guess the letters. Your parent types them.
  • It never submits without a yes. Every click it makes waits for a yes, and the final Submit is always pressed by your parent.
  • It never sends the Aadhaar number anywhere. Its optional AI help gets a masked description of the form, with no Aadhaar, OTP, name, phone or answers. The number is kept on this computer only if you agree, encrypted, and deleted after the application or 7 days.
  • No account, and no servers of ours. Settings stay in this browser and in the extension. How your data is handled

Where it works today

Who it is for

Built first for people living with Parkinson’s

A hand that shakes, movements that are slow to start, eyes that tire: each one makes a captcha box or a ward list harder. The check doesn’t ask about any of that. It measures how the mouse and the text work for this person today, and sizes the screen to match. So it works the same for anyone over 70 whose hands or eyes make these forms hard, and nothing on screen labels them.

Many people have better and worse hours, for example as a dose of medicine wears off. On a harder day, run the check again. It takes a minute and replaces the old settings.

Questions

Common questions

What is Steady?

Steady is a free Chrome helper for older people, built first for people living with Parkinson’s. After a one-minute check of tapping, steadiness and reading, it shows each step of a government health form in large text, reads it aloud in Hindi or English, and does the safe steps after a yes.

Does Steady fill in Aadhaar, OTP or captcha?

No. Aadhaar, OTPs, captchas, consent and the final Submit are always done by the person, on a large keypad inside the helper. The helper only does safe steps, such as choosing from a list, pressing Next, or trying again when the server is busy.

Where does the AI come in?

On a portal with verified steps, the helper follows them. On any other website, an optional AI planner (Claude) suggests one next step at a time from a masked description of the page: labels and whether fields are filled, never what was typed or any numbers. Guardrails block anything private.

Is my data stored or sent anywhere?

There are no servers of ours. The one-minute check stays in your browser. Answers saved on the practice portal are encrypted on the device and deleted after the application, or after 7 days. Nothing from other websites is stored.

Does it work on the real government portal today?

Not yet. Today it works fully on a practice copy of a 70+ enrolment portal, and in a lighter mode on any website you open it on. Real portals need verified steps, which are added one portal at a time after a manual check.
