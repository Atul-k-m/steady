# Hospital-run patient-access programmes in India and older adults (60+)

Scope: programmes run by hospitals, or deployed inside hospitals, in India: kiosks, QR/ABHA tokens, queue and display boards, navigators and help desks, senior counters and geriatric OPDs, WhatsApp bots, patient apps, lab-report delivery, and elder-specific schemes. Research date 2026-11-15.

Evidence labels used below:
- **[fetched]**: I read the page myself.
- **[search excerpt]**: the figure came from a search-engine summary of the page and I did not open the page. Treat it as lower confidence.
- **[vendor claim]**: a marketing case study.
- **NOT FOUND**: I looked for the data and found none. I estimated nothing.

## Key Question 1: Which hospitals run these programmes, and what usage or impact numbers do they report?

### Takeaway
The best-measured programme is the National Health Authority's ABHA "Scan & Share" (also called "Scan and Register") QR token at hospital registration counters. By 8 Aug 2026 it had logged 25 crore registrations at 30,800 facilities, and AIIMS New Delhi was the top government facility with 54.29 lakh registrations. Most other programmes publish only launch claims or one-off before/after figures: kiosks, display boards, volunteer navigators, senior counters, WhatsApp bots and patient apps. No programme found publishes usage broken down by age.

#### Peer-reviewed and other measured evidence on hospital digital registration
- **Cureus, 9 Apr 2025 (Ranjan A, Singh G, Singh H, Singh M; authors at Sarojini Naidu Medical College, Agra).** A time-motion study of 40 patients (20 online ABHA, 20 offline) between 1 and 21 Oct 2024.
  - Mean total time: 4.15 minutes online vs 10.37 minutes offline.
  - Mean waiting time: about 1 minute vs about 9 minutes.
  - The online route added about 2 minutes for app download and data entry.
  - No age breakdown and no kiosk or help desk at the site. The 50/50 split was set by the study design and is not a measure of actual usage.
  - [PMC12063933](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12063933/) [fetched]
- **BMC Health Services Research, 2026 (Agra).** 425 OPD attendees across three tertiary hospitals. ABHA adoption lagged conventional registration, and the main barriers were digital literacy and awareness. Age-wise results not seen. [Springer s12913-026-14314-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12913-026-14314-7) [search excerpt; the full text redirected to a login page]
- **Cureus (Northeast India, OB/GYN OPD).** Volunteers generate ABHA IDs at the counters for patients without smartphones. [Cureus 358589 (20 Apr 2025 upload)](https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/358589/20250420-664036-m7dvqf.pdf) [search excerpt]
- **Frontiers in Digital Health, 2024 (Tamil Nadu).** Observation of two older rural patients referred to a tertiary hospital who struggled with digitised services. This is a small qualitative observation, not a full study. [Frontiers fdgth.2024.1468633](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2024.1468633/full) [search excerpt]

### Inferences
- Only two kinds of figure are reported at scale. The NHA reports Scan & Share token counts, and individual hospitals report before/after timings. Every registration figure here is either a total for all ages or a timing measured on a convenience sample. No hospital or agency found reports what share of 60+ patients use a digital channel.
- The large wait-time claims (6 hours saved at AIIMS in 2016; 50 to 4 minutes, and 30–40 to 5–10 minutes, for Scan & Share) measure time at the registration counter. The PGIMER reports say pre-registration does not shorten the OPD wait itself [search excerpt]. For older patients, the counter may therefore be only part of the bottleneck.
- Senior-citizen counters, priority cards and geriatric clinics exist as hospital-level arrangements (Delhi, Goa, Rohtak, AIIMS Bhopal, Aster Kochi), and in law through Section 20 of the MWPSC Act. No hospital publishes data on how much they are used or whether they work.

### Gaps
- Current kiosk counts and the share of registrations done at kiosks at AIIMS New Delhi, PGIMER, Safdarjung and state medical colleges: NOT FOUND. The newest AIIMS kiosk figure (about 200 kiosks) dates from about 2016. I found no Safdarjung kiosk data.

- The full text of the 2026 BMC Agra study was behind a login, so any age-stratified results are unknown.
- Pharmacy dispensing token systems in government hospitals: beyond Delhi's separate senior dispensing queue and BSAH's separate drug counter [search excerpt], NOT FOUND.
- I found no government hospital with a named geriatric care coordinator role. The only role close to it is the escort staff at Aster Medcity, a private hospital.

## Key Question 2: What evidence shows that older patients cannot use these programmes unaided?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mostly qualitative and news-based, with no large age-stratified study. Even so, the pattern is consistent. Kiosks were designed for "literate and tech savvy patients" with staff standing by. QR/ABHA tokens assume a smartphone and mobile data. Older patients without phones fall back on offline counters, or on assisted counters that patients describe as slower. Where staffed help desks exist (PMAM desks, senior counters, priority cards), audits and news reports show they are often missing, not open 24x7, untrained, or ignored.

### Cited Findings
- **AIIMS kiosks needed staff from the start.** AIIMS's own account says Patient Care Coordinators "were also deployed to assist with the E-Kiosks to enable literate and tech savvy patients with appointments". It also describes "severe resistance from Patients, Doctors, Support Staff and Security" during the change (about 2016). — [AIIMS, V. Srinivas (about 2016)](https://www.aiims.edu/images/pdf/notice/AIIMS%20-%20THE%20FIRST%20DIGITAL%20REVOLUTION%20IN%20HEALTH%20CARE%20(1).pdf) [fetched]
- **AIIMS call centre.** An undated AIIMS note says most AIIMS patients "are illiterate and are not able to utilize the web portal/IVRS", which led to a call centre. — [AIIMS appointment system note (undated, accessed 2026-11-15)](https://aiims.edu/images/pdf/notice/Digital%20AIIMS/appointment%20system-white.pdf) [search excerpt]
- **Gandhi Hospital, Hyderabad (QR/ABHA registration).**
  - A 57-year-old patient: "The staff asked me to download the app, but I don't even have a smartphone."
  - Another patient: "We don't have internet recharge on our phone. Without that, we can't download or scan the code." She waited nearly an hour.
  - Help desks are "reportedly available", but patients often find little support at the counters.
  - Daily footfall was about 2,500.
  - — [OC Academy blog (28 Sep 2025), citing ETHealthworld/TOI](https://www.ocacademy.in/blogs/digital-opd-system-gandhi-hospital-challenges) [fetched; secondary, auto-generated summary of news reports]
- **GDMC, Dehradun.**
  - A septuagenarian: "This may land the elderly people like us who do not have a smartphone in problem."
  - Jagmohan Singh: "I feel the online system is not suitable for senior citizens, women or individuals without smartphones."

  - — [Pioneer Edge (8 Jan 2026)](https://www.pioneeredge.in/patients-urge-gdmc-hospital-not-to-make-online-opd-registration-mandatory/) [fetched]
- **ABHA pushed in practice.** Some hospitals reportedly require ABHA IDs for OPD registration even though ABHA is officially voluntary. — [OC Academy (28 Sep 2025)](https://www.ocacademy.in/blogs/digital-opd-system-gandhi-hospital-challenges) [fetched]. A Hindustan Times Patna headline reads "AIIMS makes ABHA tokens mandatory for OPD consultation". — [Magzter/HT Patna (date not visible)](https://www.magzter.com/hi/stories/newspaper/Hindustan-Times-Patna/AIIMS-MAKES-ABHA-TOKENS-MANDATORY-FOR-OPD-CONSULTATION) [search excerpt; headline only]

### Inferences
- In the sources found, every digital channel (kiosk, QR token, WhatsApp, app, SMS report) is paired with, or later had added, a human fallback: Patient Care Coordinators, PMAMs, volunteers, a PRO, an assisted ABHA counter or an offline counter. This suggests hospitals themselves expect a share of patients, older patients prominent among them, not to use the digital channel unaided. I found no number for that share.
- Where the fallback is an assisted digital counter rather than a plain offline counter, patients describe it as slower (GDMC). Pressure to make ABHA mandatory therefore lands hardest on older patients without phones.
- Reports of human help-desk roles (PMAMs in Bihar, priority cards at Rohtak, senior windows in Bengaluru in 2012) show those roles are often understaffed or not enforced. Older patients may then lose both the digital route and the human one.

### Gaps
- A systematic study measuring kiosk, QR or WhatsApp use by patients aged 60+ in Indian hospitals, or the share who need staff help: NOT FOUND.
- Observational counts (for example, kiosks idle, or staff operating kiosks on patients' behalf) from AIIMS, PGIMER or Safdarjung: NOT FOUND.
- Older users' experience with hospital WhatsApp bots, patient apps or SMS lab reports: NOT FOUND in any source.
- The Gandhi Hospital evidence comes through an auto-generated secondary blog. I did not fetch the original ETHealthworld or TOI article.

## Key Question 3: Who funds and controls these programmes?

### Takeaway
Control is split. The NHA under ABDM sets the national QR token standard and promotes it. Each hospital's medical superintendent and IT team decides whether and how to deploy it through the hospital's HMIS vendor or NIC's e-Hospital. The big early transformations were part-financed by corporate CSR (TCS at AIIMS) and NGOs (Rotary at GMCH-32). PM-JAY help desks are paid for by empanelled hospitals, or by state health agencies through outsourced facilitation agencies. Elder-specific measures rest on state duties under Section 20 of the MWPSC Act and on NHM's NPHCE money, and in practice depend on each hospital's administration. Private hospitals fund their own apps and bots, often with vendors.
