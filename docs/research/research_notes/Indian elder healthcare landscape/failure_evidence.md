# Evidence: how existing digital and service healthcare solutions in India fail older adults (60+)

Compiled 2026-11-15. India only. Each entry gives: finding, then method, sample and setting, date, journey-stage tags, and quality tier, then the source link.

Quality tiers used throughout:
- **A**: official national survey, government audit, or official programme data (LASI, CAG, parliamentary replies, NHA data)
- **B**: peer-reviewed study (B1 = multi-site or large-N quantitative; B2 = single-site or small or qualitative)
- **C**: NGO or think-tank survey or analysis (HelpAge India, ORF, NITI summaries), not peer-reviewed
- **D**: news report (single case or local data)
- **E**: blog, guide site, advocacy page, or search-snippet-only figure (weakest; verify before use)

Journey stages: S1 Find doctor · S2 Book · S3 Register · S4 Wait/navigate · S5 Consultation · S6 Tests · S7 Reports · S8 Medicines · S9 Follow-ups · S10 Payments/insurance · S11 Digital health IDs/teleconsult · S12 Emergencies · S0 Other.

## Q1a. Baseline digital and functional capability of older Indians (LASI, NSS/NFHS, NGO surveys)

### Takeaway
The only nationally representative 60+ dataset (LASI Wave 1, 2017–18) shows that 60% of older Indians are not literate (three in four women), 37% have measured low vision, and about a quarter of those with functional limitations need a helper. "Getting around in unfamiliar places" and "making telephone calls" are among the most common difficulties with everyday tasks such as shopping, phoning or managing money (IADL limitations). LASI publishes only household-level phone ownership, not individual smartphone or internet use for 60+. NGO surveys of urban seniors put comfortable digital-device use at about 1 in 5, and smartphone use at about 41%.

### Cited Findings
**Literacy, vision, hearing, need for help (LASI Wave 1, quality A)**
- **Literacy, 60+:** 39.6% are literate (men 56.6%, women 24.2%; rural 30.0%, urban 62.5%). 56.5% have no schooling (men 38.6%, women 72.7%). Method: national household survey. Sample: 31,902 respondents aged 60+, all states and UTs. Data 2017–18; factsheet published 2021 (IIPS/MoHFW). Stages: S0, S1–S3, S11. Quality A. Note: I read the rural and urban columns from a PDF table whose layout was garbled; the literate rural/urban values are consistent with the no-schooling rural/urban values (65.9% / 34.1%) in the same table. — [LASI W1 Factsheet: Elderly in India](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf)
- **Need for a helper:** 24.8% of people aged 60+ with ADL/IADL limitations need a helper (urban 27.9%, rural 23.8%). 48.0% have at least one IADL limitation (women 56.7%, men 38.3%; rural 51.4%, urban 39.7%). 23.8% have at least one ADL limitation. Method, sample and date as above. Stages: S4, S5, S8, S12. Quality A. — [LASI W1 Factsheet: Elderly in India](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf)
- **Most common IADL limitation:** Among people aged 60+, "getting around in unfamiliar places" is the most common IADL limitation (the text states this). In the executive summary chart (Figure 38), my reading of the bar values puts it at about 29.8%, with "making telephone calls" at about 28.6%, "managing money" about 27.7% and "taking medication" about 15.7%. The chart labels were garbled in extraction, so I matched each value to its label by bar order; only the "most common" ranking is stated in the text. Data 2017–18; published 2020–21. Stages: S2, S4, S8, S11. Quality A. — [LASI Wave 1 India Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf)
- **Vision:** 37% of people aged 60+ have measured low vision (low near or low distance), against 26% at 45–59. Low near vision is 32% and low distance vision 15%. Low vision is 43% among the elderly with no education. 55% report any eye problem. Method: measured tumbling-E logMAR chart plus self-report. Stages: S3, S7, S8, S11. Quality A. — [LASI Wave 1 India Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf)
- **Hearing, aids and other limits:**
  - 9.6% of people aged 60+ report hearing or ear problems, but only 0.7% use a hearing aid (rural 0.6%, urban 1.1%).
  - 37.4% use spectacles or contact lenses (rural 30.0%, urban 55.7%).
  - Health insurance coverage among people aged 60+ is 18.2% (pre-PM-JAY expansion).
  - Stages: S5, S11 (audio teleconsults), S10. Quality A. — [LASI W1 Factsheet: Elderly in India](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf)
- **Impairment:** 11% of people aged 60+ report at least one impairment (locomotor 5.9%, visual 4.1%, mental 3%). Visual and hearing impairment is higher in rural areas. Stages: S4, S5. Quality A. — [LASI Wave 1 India Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf)

**Phone ownership (household level only)**
- **LASI households:** 87% of LASI households (households with a member aged 45+) have a mobile phone. This is household ownership, not the older person's own use. Data 2017–18. Stages: S2, S11. Quality A. — [LASI Wave 1 India Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf)
- **Urban–rural split (via ORF):** ORF, citing LASI, reports household mobile access of 93.6% urban versus 84.1% rural for households aged 45+. Method: secondary analysis or commentary. Quality C (citing A). — [ORF, "Screen Time May Be Rising Steadily Among Older Indians"](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/screen-time-may-be-rising-steadily-among-older-indians)

- **Older cancer patients:** ORF cites a study in which 81% of older Indian cancer patients used mobile phones but only 25% used the internet. Use was lower among those with no education or impaired cognition. Underlying study c. 2021. **NGO surveys (quality C)**
- **HelpAge India "Ageing in India" (June 2024), digital devices:**
  - Only 1 in 5 elderly respondents can use digital devices comfortably; the other four cannot use them at all or need continuous support.
  - Device access: men 48% versus women 33%; only 26% of those aged 80+ have access to any digital device.
  - Use is mainly for entertainment and social media (34%).
  - Method: survey. Sample: 5,169 elders plus 1,333 caregivers, 20 Tier I/II cities in 10 states, SEC B and C households only (urban, excludes rural and the poorest). Stages: S1, S2, S11. Quality C. — [HelpAge India National Salient Findings 2024](https://helpageindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/NATIONAL-SALIENT-FINDINGS-HelpAge-India-Report-2024.pdf); [Full report](https://helpageindia.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/REPORT-Ageing-in-India-Exploring-Preparedness-Response-to-Care-Challenges-A-HelpAge-India-Report.pdf)
- **HelpAge India "Understanding Intergenerational Dynamics & Perceptions on Ageing" (13 June 2025):**
  - 71% of elders find basic feature phones easy to use, but only 41% use or own smartphones; Outlook reports 40%.
  - Only 13% engage with social media or the internet.
  - 66% find digital tools "too confusing", and 51% avoid digital modes for fear of making errors.
  - Children (54%) and grandchildren (52%) are the main people teaching them.
  - Method: survey. Sample: 5,798 respondents in 10 cities, about 30% of them aged 60+. Stages: S1, S2, S11. Quality C. — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/news/digital-divide-among-elderly-66-find-digital-tools-too-confusing); [The Week/PTI, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/06/13/des19-elderly-study-loneliness.html)
  - **Correction:** a Deccan Herald headline, "88% seniors offline", misreads this report. Per a secondary summary, the 88% refers to intergenerational communication being face-to-face, not to elders being offline. Do not cite 88% as an offline rate. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2F88-seniors-offline-helpage-urges-action-3585484)

**Small local studies (quality B2 / E)**
- **Rural Karnataka (DAHLIA):** In a study of 150 older adults, 50% owned a mobile phone and very few owned or used a smartphone; digital and health literacy were both low. Published c. 2022. Quality B2: figures from the abstract/snippet; full text not verified. — [DAHLIA, PMC8938771](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8938771/)
- **Systematic review:** A review of Indian studies concludes that internet use and eHealth literacy are low among the Indian elderly, with a significant rural–urban digital divide. Published c. 2024. Quality B (snippet only). — [Digital literacy among older adults: a systematic review in Indian context](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383920363_Digital_literacy_among_older_adults_a_systematic_review_in_Indian_context)

### Inferences
- LASI pairs 60.4% non-literacy (75.8% among women) with "making telephone calls" and "getting around in unfamiliar places" as top IADL difficulties. So any text-, OTP- or app-based step, and any unfamiliar hospital layout, are mismatched with a large share of the 60+ population before smartphone access is even considered.
- The gender gap shows up in every source: literacy (24.2% women vs 56.6% men in LASI) and device access (33% vs 48% in HelpAge 2024).
- Nearly all smartphone and internet figures for 60+ come from urban NGO samples or conflicting secondary summaries. No nationally representative individual-level figure was found.

### Gaps
- **LASI individual internet and smartphone use, 60+:** NOT FOUND. The LASI W1 executive summary and the Elderly factsheet report only household phone ownership.
- **LASI Wave 2:** An IIPS national report is NOT FOUND. Only LASI-DAD (dementia sub-study) Wave 2 data was released, in Feb 2025 ([Gateway to Global Aging](https://g2aging.org/additional-resources/gateway-blog/b18e71fb-03b9-47de-9dcd-7182518c8c1b)).
- **NFHS-5:** Its internet-use indicators cover only women and men aged 15–49, so there are no 60+ figures (structural gap; I did not re-verify this session).
- **NSS 78th round / CAMS:** Tables of ICT and digital-literacy skills for people aged 60+ were NOT FOUND in this search.
- **Primary NITI Aayog figure (13% vs under 3%):** unresolved.

## Q1b. Telemedicine and eSanjeevani: use by older patients, satisfaction and barriers

### Takeaway
eSanjeevani is used mainly through the provider-assisted model at Health and Wellness Centres, which accounts for over 93% of use. Programme data say people aged 60+ use it less than adults aged 25–45, but publish no 60+ share. Qualitative work with rural elderly (Jharkhand) documents audio and video lag, dialect mismatch, unreachable doctors and dependence on community health officers (CHOs). The Kerala survey excluded people aged 70+ and found older users rated the platform harder to use.

### Cited Findings
- **National programme data:**
  - Over 163 million eSanjeevani consultations from Nov 2019 to Sep 2023. The assisted AB-HWC model accounted for over 93% of use.
  - Adults aged 25–45 were the largest user group: on the patient-direct OPD platform their share rose from 50.7% (2020) to 62.2% (2023); on AB-HWC it rose from 36.7% to over 42%.
  - Children and adults aged 60+ used the platform less; no 60+ percentage is published.
  - Data inconsistency: the paper's text and Table 3 give different top-5 states.
  - Method: programmatic data analysis (C-DAC and Intelehealth authors). Published 7 Oct 2025, Oxford Open Digital Health. Stages: S11, S5. Quality A/B1 (official data in a peer-reviewed journal, authored by the platform's builders). — [Sood et al., PMC12558045](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558045/)
- **Rural Jharkhand, elderly patients:**
  - Elderly patients did not recognise the name "e-Sanjeevani" even though it was painted on the HWC wall; they saw the CHO as their doctor.
  - Observers recorded login failures, audio-visual problems and glitches, which pushed consultations to asynchronous mode. One patient (F, 60) said: "The video was lagging and stuttering… couldn't understand."
  - Patients could not converse with doctors in their local dialect (Sadri); a Hindi mishearing ("khaansi" vs "khaati") caused a miscommunication.
  - Doctors were often busy or offline. One patient (M, 69) waited over 30 minutes without the call connecting.
  - There is no appointment-scheduling feature. CHOs pay about Rs 300 a month for data. Patients sometimes pay out of pocket for diagnostics or medicines that facilities lack.
  - Method: qualitative (in-depth interviews plus observation). Sample: 27 participants, including 21 elderly aged 60–85 (10 with no formal education), at AB-HWCs, CHCs and villages; fieldwork Jun–Nov 2023. Published 30 Aug 2025, Health and Population: Perspectives and Issues. Stages: S2, S5, S6, S8, S11. Quality B2. — [Shalini & Thakur, HPPI 2025](https://www.hppi.in/abstractArticleContentBrowse/HPPI/41486/JPJ/fullText)
- **Kerala beneficiary survey:**
  - 75% were satisfied.
  - Younger users (<45) were more likely to find eSanjeevani easy to use than older users. Men, the employed and college-educated reported greater ease (p<0.001 for education and employment).
  - Reported challenges: technical or connectivity 13.6%, interface problems 10.9%, longer waiting times 7.5%.
  - Among non-users: 36.2% preferred in-person care, and 21.3% did not know the service was still running.
  - Method: survey plus key-informant interviews. Sample: 300 beneficiaries in Thiruvananthapuram, stratified at 60 per age band from 20–29 to 60–69, so **people aged 70+ were excluded**. The sample was highly educated (50.7% graduates, 27.3% postgraduates). Fieldwork Oct–Nov 2023; published Oct 2025, IJCMPH 12(10):4572–80. Stages: S11, S5. Quality B2. — [Rosh et al., IJCMPH 2025](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/14324/8698/72209)
- **Scoping review:** Limited digital literacy, especially among older adults and in rural areas, is a central individual-level barrier to telemedicine. Other barriers: limited smartphone access, unreliable internet, preference for in-person consultation. Family involvement is a facilitator. Method: scoping review. Published 21 Aug 2024, Cureus. Stages: S11. Quality B. — [Scoping review, PMC11414145](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11414145/)
- **Aligarh mixed-methods study:** Adoption among older adults is limited by accessibility, digital literacy, cost and ethical concerns. Digital literacy and family support were independent predictors of empowerment. Sample: 200 adults aged 60+ surveyed, plus 20 elderly patients and 10 professionals interviewed. Published 2025, BMC Geriatrics. Stages: S11. Quality B2 (details from search snippet). — [BMC Geriatrics 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-025-06872-y)

### Inferences
- The "success" of eSanjeevani at scale rests on the assisted model (93%+). Independent, patient-initiated teleconsultation by older adults is marginal on the available evidence.
- Audio quality and dialect barriers documented in Jharkhand combine with LASI's 9.6% self-reported hearing problems and 0.7% hearing-aid use. Together these suggest audio-only or low-bandwidth consults are especially fragile for older users. This is an inference; there is no direct measurement.

### Gaps
- **Share of eSanjeevani consultations by people aged 60+:** NOT FOUND. Programme papers say only that use is "less".
- **Satisfaction of patients aged 70+ with eSanjeevani:** NOT FOUND (the Kerala study capped at 69).
- **Quantified need for a family member during teleconsults among 60+:** NOT FOUND.
- **Teleconsult hearing or audio failure rates specific to older users:** NOT FOUND.

## Q1c. ABHA, ABDM and Scan & Share: enrolment and use barriers

### Takeaway
Hospital studies (2024–26) show low ABHA conversion: about 16% of OPD footfall linked at AIIMS Kalyani, and 6% self-created accounts at RIMS Ranchi. The dominant failures are no network signal, no smartphone, OTP hesitancy, language and privacy fear. Knowledge of ABHA declines with age, and adults aged 80+ face more usability barriers. No study isolates the 60+ group with exact percentages.

### Cited Findings
- **Agra tertiary hospitals:**
  - Over 537,278 OPD visits in eight months, 129,007 ABHA IDs were created, a monthly adoption rate of 23.96% (below paper-registration levels).
  - Barriers: preference for in-person care 85.6%, high data cost 81.9%, difficulty using health apps 65.6%, language difficulty 64.0%, hesitation to share an OTP 60.0%.
  - 88% said they need training; 58.6% lacked adequate ABDM knowledge; only 55% felt comfortable with data privacy.
  - Age correlated weakly and negatively with digital health literacy (ρ = −0.230). Per the search summary, adults aged 80+ faced more digital-literacy and usability barriers.
  - Method: analytical cross-sectional study. Sample: 425 OPD attendees, 3 tertiary hospitals in Agra, Sep 2024–Apr 2025. Published 5 Mar 2026, BMC Health Services Research 26:512. Stages: S3, S11. Quality B1. — [Ranjan et al., PMC13078079](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC13078079); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-14314-7)
- **AIIMS Kalyani (West Bengal) OPD:**
  - Of 80,497 OPD footfall, 30,786 ABHA tokens were generated and 13,085 linkages completed.
  - Of 5,873 recorded failure events: no network signal 41.3%, no smartphone 30.7%, patient refusal 22.2%, miscellaneous (server downtime, technical errors, Aadhaar-mobile issues) 5.8%.
  - No age-stratified results.
  - Method: prospective observational audit, 19 Jun–29 Nov 2024. Published 18 Feb 2026, Cureus 18(2):e103882. Stages: S3, S11. Quality B2. — [Dagar et al., PMC13005048](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13005048/)
- **RIMS Ranchi (Jharkhand) OPD:**
  - 46.8% had heard of ABHA; 16.0% knew it can be created online; 6.0% had personally created an account.
  - Age was the only variable significantly associated with knowledge, with a graded decline as age rose (χ²(4)=15.795, p=0.003).
  - 73.8% had a high perceived-barrier score. Privacy or data misuse was the most-endorsed barrier, followed by OTP and authentication problems and device or internet limitations.
  - 79.5% said a hospital helpdesk would increase enrolment.
  - The oldest age band was 50+ (27.5%); there is no 60+ breakdown.
  - Method: cross-sectional, convenience sample. Sample: 400 adults, May–Jul 2026. Published 6 Oct 2026, Cureus 18(10):e117541. Stages: S3, S11. Quality B2. — [Sharan et al., PMC13738156](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13738156)
- **Community study:** Most participants had no awareness of ABDM or the ABHA app; 78.3% cited fear of misuse of Aadhaar-linked data. Method: community cross-sectional study, IJCMPH; date and sample not verified. Stages: S11. Quality B2/E (snippet only). — [IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/download/15784/9457/81212)
- **Scan & Share (official and trade descriptions):**
  - Scan & Share requires a smartphone with the ABHA, Aarogya Setu or a similar app to scan a hospital QR code. Patients without one use an ABHA card QR or hospital kiosks.
  - Registration time is claimed to fall from about 50 minutes to 4–5 minutes. Facility and token counts vary widely between sources (for example, 28,818 facilities, and both 2.27 crore and 23.21 crore tokens).
  - Method: official and trade descriptions. Stages: S3, S4, S11. Quality D/E. — [Digital Health News](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/scan-share-how-a-simple-qr-code-eliminated-hospital-queues-for-millions); [WHO Bulletin, "Scan and share to register outpatients, India", PMC11774221](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11774221/)
- **AIIMS Delhi online booking (news):** Some patients lack the Aadhaar-linked phones needed for OTP-based online booking, and rural families often could not read the OTPs. Method: ground report. Published 21 Apr 2023. Stages: S2, S3, S11. Quality D. — [Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2023/04/21/queue-for-queues-packed-online-slots-and-the-endless-wait-to-be-treated-at-aiims-delhi)

### Inferences
- In the AIIMS Kalyani audit, network signal plus no smartphone account for 72% of failures. That is consistent with LASI and HelpAge data showing limited personal device ownership among elders, but the study did not record age.
- ABHA linkage depends on an Aadhaar-linked mobile and OTP, so the older person often depends on whoever holds that phone. Direct quantification of "OTP goes to a child's phone" was not found.

### Gaps
- **ABHA enrolment rate among people aged 60+ (official, by age):** NOT FOUND.
- **Share of elderly whose Aadhaar-linked mobile belongs to a family member:** NOT FOUND.
- **Scan & Share use or failure rates by age:** NOT FOUND.
- **Studies of consent-flow comprehension (ABDM consent manager) among older adults:** NOT FOUND.

## Q1d. Online appointment systems (AIIMS ORS, state online OPD) and touts or queues

### Takeaway
At AIIMS Delhi, online slots in high-demand departments were reported booked out for months to a year. Overnight queues persisted after online booking: by January 2026 the hospital had opened an official shelter (Ashray) for people waiting overnight. Evidence is news-based. No study measures elderly patients' use of, or failure with, ORS.

### Cited Findings
- **AIIMS Delhi, Newslaundry ground report:**
  - Online slots were booked out: general medicine, orthopaedics and gynaecology fully booked "for the year"; some patients reported waits of up to a year (orthopaedics about four months).
  - Hundreds of patients camp outside the gate overnight; "unofficial" slots are decided at night; Tatkal OPD runs only 8–11 am. When it rains, the queue moves to the parking area, and there are no nearby toilets.
  - Gita Devi, 60, from Darbhanga (Bihar), arrived at about 6:30 am to be told all appointments were booked; she was still wandering the OPD at noon.
  - The online portal covered 21 departments. About 10,000 OPD patients a day. 363 of 1,207 sanctioned faculty posts were vacant.
  - A November 2022 ransomware attack left reports (MRI, CT, biopsy) hard to retrieve two months later.
  - Method: ground report. Published 21 Apr 2023. Stages: S2, S3, S4, S7. Quality D. — [Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2023/04/21/queue-for-queues-packed-online-slots-and-the-endless-wait-to-be-treated-at-aiims-delhi)
- **AIIMS Delhi "Ashray" facility:** In January 2026 AIIMS New Delhi opened Ashray for patients waiting overnight for OPD services. Night-time waiters are transported there and given tokens that keep their order for next-morning registration; capacity is about 250 places. Method: official broadcaster news. Published 4 Jan 2026. Stages: S2, S4. Quality A/D (official broadcaster; details from search snippet, page fetch failed). — [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/aiims-new-delhi-operationalises-ashray-facility-for-patients-waiting-overnight-for-opd-services)
- **Political complaint:** An AAP leader raised continuing overnight OPD queues at AIIMS in 2026. Exact date not verified. Stages: S4. Quality D. — [DNP India](https://www.dnpindia.in/health/anurag-dhanda-raises-questions-over-aiims-opd-queues-highlights-patients-waiting-overnight/616084/)
- **Bribes for appointments, RML Hospital:** In May 2024 the CBI arrested 11 people at RML Hospital, Delhi, including two cardiologists, over alleged bribes from patients for "fixing their appointments" with doctors (and from suppliers). The CBI had arrested a Safdarjung Hospital doctor in a similar case the previous year. This concerns paid access to appointments, not specifically online ORS slots or elderly patients. Method: news on an enforcement action. Stages: S2, S10. Quality D. — [News on AIR](https://newsonair.gov.in/cbi-arrests-11-people-in-medical-nexus-at-delhis-rml-hospital)
- **Phone and online share one pool:** Guide sites say phone and online bookings share one slot pool, and that online appointments still require same-day registration. Stages: S2, S3. Quality E. — [govhospitals.com guide](https://govhospitals.com/government-hospitals-blog/aiims-delhi-online-appointment/)

### Inferences
- Online booking did not remove the physical overnight queue at AIIMS Delhi. The 2026 Ashray shelter is institutional confirmation that overnight waiting continues. Older patients lacking Aadhaar-linked phones or OTP literacy are pushed into that queue by default (inference from the Newslaundry and LASI evidence).

### Gaps
- **Independent evidence of touts gaming or reselling ORS online slots:** NOT FOUND.
- **Share of ORS bookings made by or for people aged 60+, or OTP failure rates:** NOT FOUND.
- **State online OPD systems (e.g., PGIMER Chandigarh, state e-Hospital) elderly-specific failure reports:** NOT FOUND.
- **Counts of elderly patients in overnight queues:** NOT FOUND.

## Q1e. Ayushman Vay Vandana card (PM-JAY 70+): enrolment, e-KYC and hospital acceptance

### Takeaway
Two years after launch (29 Oct 2024), official figures show about 1.14 crore cards (28 Feb 2026) against an eligible pool of about 6 crore. That is roughly 19% coverage, a figure calculated by a news outlet. In Nagpur an RTI reply showed 8.25% coverage. Reported enrolment barriers are Aadhaar-mobile non-linkage, fingerprint failure on ageing hands, fees at kiosks, and hospital suspensions over unpaid dues. Most of this evidence is local news.

### Cited Findings
- **Cards and admissions, official figures** (Method: official parliamentary data; Stages: S10; Quality A):
  - 14 lakh cards by 25 Nov 2024 (Lok Sabha written reply). — [News on AIR, Nov 2024](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/14-lakh-ayushman-vay-vandana-cards-created-for-senior-citizens-aged-70-years-and-above-under-ab-pmjay)
  - Over 93 lakh cards by 5 Dec 2025, and 7.89 lakh authorised admissions worth Rs 1,741 crore by 31 Oct 2025 (Lok Sabha reply, Dec 2025, as reported). Reporting outlet is partisan; figures are attributed to the parliamentary reply. — [Organiser, 4 Nov 2026](https://organiser.org/2026/11/04/424029/bharat/two-years-of-ayushman-vay-vandana-how-a-health-scheme-under-modi-govt-is-transforming-healthcare-for-senior-citizens/)

## Q1f. CAG performance audit of PM-JAY (Report No. 11 of 2023) and other audits

### Takeaway
The CAG audit (Sep 2018–Mar 2021, 28 States/UTs, tabled 8 Aug 2023) documents system-level failures that affect access for any beneficiary: corrupt beneficiary data, thin hospital supply, beneficiaries charged despite cover, empanelled hospitals providing no treatment, and grievance redressal that met its deadline in under 10% of cases. The executive summary contains no elderly-specific finding.

### Cited Findings
All findings in this section come from the CAG executive summary. Method: performance audit. Period: Sep 2018–Mar 2021, 28 States/UTs. Quality A. Sources: [CAG Report No. 11 of 2023, Executive Summary (PDF)](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2023/04_Executive-Summary-064d22bab3b3f45.25634714.pdf) and [CAG report page](https://cag.gov.in/cen/delhi-v/en/audit-report/details/119060).
- **Registration:** 7.87 crore households were registered, 73% of the 10.74 crore targeted (as of Nov 2022). Stages: S3, S10.
- **Beneficiary data errors:** invalid names, unrealistic dates of birth, duplicate PM-JAY IDs. In Tamil Nadu, 4,761 registrations were made against 7 Aadhaar numbers. Between 11 and 7,49,820 beneficiaries were registered against the same or an invalid mobile number in the Beneficiary Identification System. Stages: S3, S10, S11.
- **Transaction system:** the system (TMS) had invalid or null patient-age entries and allowed pre-authorisation requests for beneficiaries shown as "died". Stages: S10.
- **Hospital supply:**
  - Empanelled hospitals per lakh beneficiaries ranged from 1.8 (Bihar) to 26.6 (Goa); it was very low in Maharashtra (3), Assam (3.4) and Rajasthan (3.8).
  - In Assam, 13 empanelled hospitals provided only 4–80% of their available facilities to PM-JAY beneficiaries.
  - Some empanelled hospitals provided no treatment at all: 524 in Andhra Pradesh, 59 in Jharkhand, 40 in Uttar Pradesh, 19 in Tamil Nadu, 5 in Punjab.
  - Stages: S1, S10, S12.
- **Beneficiaries charged despite cover:** beneficiaries were charged for treatment in empanelled hospitals in Meghalaya (13,418), J&K (459), Himachal Pradesh (50) and Jharkhand (36), raising out-of-pocket spending. Stages: S10.
- **Claims delays:** 39.57 lakh claims took longer than the specified 12 hours for pre-authorisation-related processing. Stages: S10.
- **Awareness (IEC):** an IEC cell was not formed in 12 States/UTs; an IEC plan existed in only 4 States. In 14 States/UTs, IEC spending was 0–20.24% of budget against a 25% benchmark. Stages: S1, S10.
- **Grievance redressal:** of 37,903 grievances, only 3,718 (9.80%) were redressed within the turnaround time and 33,100 (87.33%) beyond it. 53.38% of appeals were resolved late. SHA Chhattisgarh redressed none of its 40 grievances. Stages: S10, S0.
- **Follow-up:** The Public Accounts Committee's 151st Report (April 2024) is based on this CAG audit. Its contents were not reviewed in this search. Quality A. — [PAC 151st Report, eparlib](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2976827/1/17_Public_Accounts_151.pdf)
- **Secondary coverage:** Down To Earth covered the audit's empanelment-delay and standards findings. Quality D. — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/delay-in-empanelling-hospitals-failure-to-meet-standards-plague-pmjay-scheme-cag-91133)

### Inferences
- The audit predates the 70+ expansion (Oct 2024). But the failures it documents (shared or invalid mobile numbers, sparse empanelment, beneficiaries charged, slow grievances) are the infrastructure that Vay Vandana card holders now depend on.
- Grievances redressed on time in 9.8% of cases means a senior who is refused or overcharged has weak recourse, by the audit's own data.

## Q1g. Private health app experience for older Indians (usability studies, review themes)

### Takeaway

No published systematic analysis of app-store reviews by older Indian users was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Heuristic evaluation:** A card-sorting exercise with 25 older adults identified four core goals: book a nearby doctor appointment, consult online, order medicines, book health check-ups. Seven expert evaluators then ran a heuristic evaluation of existing Indian healthcare apps against older-adult personas. The detailed findings are paywalled and were not accessed. Published 2025, New Review of Hypermedia and Multimedia 32(1). Stages: S1, S2, S8, S6, S11. Quality B2. — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13614568.2025.2572573)
- **Pune SEM survey:** Perceived ease of use and health literacy, beyond digital capability, predicted continued mHealth app use. Method: SEM survey. Sample: 179 community-dwelling older adults in Pune, mean age 73.5 ± 5.05. Published c. 2025. Stages: S11. Quality B2 (abstract/snippet only). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396300914_Perceived_ease_of_use_and_health_literacy_as_determinants_of_mHealth_app_usage_among_older_adults_in_India_a_SEM_approach)
- **Scoping review:** A review of usability challenges in mHealth technologies for community-dwelling older adults, published in the Indian Journal of Gerontology. Its evidence base is not India-specific. Stages: S11. ## Q1h. Elder-specific barriers in physical hospital visits (navigation, queues, distance, escorts, accompaniment burden)

### Takeaway
LASI shows that older adults travel an average of about 14.5 km for outpatient and about 43.6 km for inpatient care. About a quarter of those with functional limits need a helper, and "getting around in unfamiliar places" is their most common IADL difficulty. Negative waiting-time ratings are nearly three times higher in public than private outpatient care (9.5% vs 3.5%). No peer-reviewed study quantifying escort burden or wage loss for adult children accompanying elderly patients was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Distance (LASI Wave 1):**
  - Older adults travelled on average 9 miles (14.54 km) for outpatient care and 27 miles (43.62 km) for inpatient care.
  - Outpatient use falls with distance (17% for facilities 11–30 km away, 10% for 30 km or more).
  - About 19% of rural older adults had to travel at least 60 km for inpatient care.
  - A corrigendum to this paper exists ([PMC12926031](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12926031/)).
  - Method: secondary analysis of LASI Wave 1. Sample: 31,902 older adults. Published April 2025, Lancet Regional Health – Southeast Asia, Vol 37, 100579. Stages: S1, S4, S12. Quality B1 (on A data; figures from search snippet). — ["Miles to go before I seek", PMC12230409](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12230409/)
- **Experience of care (LASI Wave 1, age 45+):**
  - Negative (bad or very bad) waiting-time ratings for outpatient care were 9.5% at public facilities versus 3.5% at private facilities; 4.6% overall.
  - Negative experiences were generally higher at public facilities.
  - Age bands were 45–54, 55–64, 65–74 and 75+. The authors describe a monotonic rise with age, but their regression tables do not clearly support it (for example, outpatient "provider of choice" AOR for 75+ = 0.73).
  - Method: secondary analysis. Sample: 33,724 outpatients and 4,330 inpatients. Published 22 Aug 2024, Public Health in Practice 8:100541. Stages: S4, S5. Quality B1. — [Ambade, Kim & Subramanian, PMC11413678](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11413678/)
- **Facility choice, cost and quality ratings (LASI executive summary, age 45+):**
  - Only 23% of adults aged 45+ use public facilities for outpatient care, against 64% private.
  - Mean out-of-pocket cost of the last outpatient visit for people aged 60+ is Rs 1,317 (public Rs 824, private Rs 1,636). Published 2020–21; data 2017–18.
  - 58% rated their last hospitalisation as good and 19% as poor; 61% rated outpatient care as good.
  - Stages: S4, S10. Quality A. — [LASI Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf); [LASI Elderly factsheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf)
- **Need for a helper (LASI):** 24.8% of people aged 60+ with ADL/IADL limitations need a helper. "Getting around in unfamiliar places" is the most common IADL limitation (about 29.8%, read from the chart). Stages: S4, S0 (escort need). Quality A. — [LASI Elderly factsheet](https://www.iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/other_files/LASI_W1_Elderly_India.pdf); [LASI Executive Summary](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Executive_Summary_0.pdf)
- **Cost of repeat trips (AIIMS Delhi case):** Sandhya Patel, a tailor from Unnao earning about Rs 200 a day, spends about Rs 3,000 per round trip to AIIMS Delhi and has made the trip for seven years. Because the AIIMS ultrasound slot was months away, she paid for a private scan. Method: news case. Published 21 Apr 2023. Stages: S4, S6, S9, S10. Quality D. — [Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2023/04/21/queue-for-queues-packed-online-slots-and-the-endless-wait-to-be-treated-at-aiims-delhi)
- **North Indian medical OPD satisfaction survey:** Satisfaction with waiting time was only moderate. The authors note a need to address the particular needs of elderly patients and women. Method: cross-sectional satisfaction survey. Published c. 2025. Stages: S4. Quality B2 (snippet). — [PMC12488123](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12488123/)
- **Western Indian tertiary hospitals:** Patients aged 45–64 were among those associated with longer OPD waits. Method: waiting-time study, two hospitals. Published c. 2021. Stages: S4. Quality B2 (snippet). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349622625_The_long_wait_for_Health_in_India-A_study_of_waiting_time_for_patients_in_a_tertiary_care_hospital_in_Western_India)
- **OPD delay causes, North India:** Delay is driven mainly by process and layout design rather than clinical care time. Method: mixed-method, stage-wise OPD flow study at a government tertiary teaching hospital. Published c. 2026. Stages: S3, S4. Quality B2 (snippet). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/417255185_Stage-wise_analysis_of_outpatient_department_patient_flow_and_waiting_time_at_a_government_tertiary_teaching_hospital_in_North_India_a_mixed-method_study)
- **Qualitative evidence synthesis:** Includes an older person's statement of having nobody to go with them to hospital. Qualitative only, no prevalence. Method: qualitative evidence synthesis on social isolation and loneliness among older Indians, IJMR. Stages: S0 (escort), S4. Quality B2. — [IJMR](https://ijmr.org.in/social-isolation-and-loneliness-among-older-persons-in-india-a-qualitative-evidence-synthesis/)
- **Families outside Delhi hospitals (advocacy):** An advocacy page says 3,000+ patient families live on footpaths outside Delhi hospitals, and most caregivers are daily-wage earners losing about Rs 300–500 a day. Not peer-reviewed; figures unverified. Stages: S4, S0. Quality E. — [Nourish Future](https://nourishfuture.org/invisible-patients-families-footpaths/)

### Inferences
- Older patients typically need a companion: a quarter of those with limitations need a helper, unfamiliar places are the top IADL difficulty, and average outpatient travel is about 14.5 km. That makes each hospital visit a two-person cost. However, the companion's lost wages are unmeasured in the peer-reviewed literature found.
- Public outpatient care has roughly 2.7 times the negative waiting-time rating of private care (9.5% vs 3.5%). Poorer elders who depend on public facilities therefore bear the worst waiting experience.

- **Studies of hospital signage, wayfinding or walking distance for older patients in Indian hospitals:** NOT FOUND.
- **LASI tabulation of forgone care with "no one to accompany" as a reason:** NOT FOUND in this search.
- **Standing time in queues for people aged 60+:** NOT FOUND.

### Inferences
- Without official age-disaggregated failure data, evidence of exclusion has to be built by triangulation: capability deficits (LASI), plus system failures (CAG, single-site ABHA audits), plus coverage shortfalls (Vay Vandana figures), plus news case evidence. This limitation should be stated explicitly in any synthesis.
