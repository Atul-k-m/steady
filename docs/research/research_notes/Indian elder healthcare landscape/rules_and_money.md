## Key Question 1: What obligations exist, and is there evidence of compliance or non-compliance by health sites and hospitals?

### Takeaway
Hospitals and health websites/apps face four overlapping sets of obligations:
- The RPwD Act 2016: Sections 40, 42, 44-46 and 89, with Rule 15 incorporating GIGW, IS 17802 and the MoHFW "Accessibility Standards for Healthcare".
- GIGW 3.0 (WCAG 2.1 AA) for government websites and apps.
- Section 20 of the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens (MWPSC) Act 2007 for government and government-funded hospitals.
- NABH accreditation standards, which are voluntary but tied to PM-JAY and insurer payment incentives.

All four are weakly enforced.
- In November 2024 the Supreme Court struck down Rule 15(1) as "recommendatory guidelines, under the garb of mandatory rules". As of 2026-11-15 the replacement mandatory ICT rules are still a draft (July 2026).
- Compliance evidence for health platforms is mostly non-compliance: the CoWIN audit was ordered in 2021 with no published outcome; Practo's own audit found critical failures on 7 of 9 iOS screens; central hospitals in Delhi received CCPD notices; and the Ayushman Bharat portal claimed only WCAG Level A.
- No audit specific to ABHA, eSanjeevani or ORS, and no academic WCAG audit of Indian hospital websites, was found.
- The RPwD framework does not treat old age as disability. The only explicit references to elderly persons are through the incorporated "Harmonised Guidelines ... for Persons with Disabilities and Elderly Persons" and through NABH's definition of "vulnerable patient".

### Cited Findings

#### 1A. RPwD Act 2016: provisions as recited by the Supreme Court (primary)
- **Section 40:** The Central Government "shall", in consultation with the Chief Commissioner, make rules laying down accessibility standards for "physical environment, transportation, information and communication including appropriate technologies and systems, and other facilities and services provided to the public in urban and rural areas". The Court held that "shall" makes rule-making mandatory (para 53). — [Rajive Raturi v. Union of India, 2024 INSC 858, judgment dated 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Section 42 (ICT):** The appropriate government shall take measures to ensure that:
  - all content in audio, print and electronic media is in accessible format;
  - persons with disabilities can use electronic media through audio description, sign language interpretation and close captioning;
  - everyday electronic goods and equipment are available in universal design (para 55).
  - [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Section 44:** No building permission, completion certificate or occupation is allowed unless the building adheres to the Section 40 rules (para 56). — [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Section 45:** Existing public buildings must be made accessible within 5 years of notification of the rules. Governments and local authorities must publish a prioritised action plan for buildings providing essential services, "such as all primary health centres, civil hospitals, schools, railway stations and bus stops" (para 57). — [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Section 46:** "All service providers – both Government and private – shall provide services in accordance with the rules on accessibility within a period of two years from the date of notification of such rules." The Central Government may extend this for categories of services (para 58). — [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Section 89 (penalty):** A fine of up to ₹10,000 for the first contravention of the Act or its rules, and ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh for subsequent contraventions (para 59). — [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
  - A secondary guide describes imprisonment up to 6 months for this section. That conflicts with the judgment, which describes only fines; prefer the judgment.
- **"Appropriate Government" (Section 2(b)):** The Central Government for establishments "wholly or substantially financed" by it, and the State Government for state-financed establishments and local authorities (footnote 57). — [SC judgment, 8 Nov 2024, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **"Private establishment" (Section 2(v)):** "a company, firm, cooperative or other society, associations, trust, agency, institution, organisation, union, factory or such other establishment as the appropriate Government may, by notification, specify". — [India Code PDF, snippet only (403 on fetch), accessed 2026-11-15](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19406/1/rpwd_act_2016.pdf)
- **Section 25 (healthcare):** Includes "barrier-free access in all parts of Government and private hospitals/health centres". — [IDR Online primer, secondary, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://idronline.org/article/rights/a-primer-on-indias-disability-law/)

#### 1B. RPwD Rules 2017: Rule 15 and the standards it incorporates
- **Commencement:** The RPwD Rules came into force on 15 June 2017 (G.S.R. 591(E)). — [SC judgment para 60, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Original Rule 15(1):** "Every establishment shall comply with" the following standards:
  - (a) the Harmonised Guidelines and Space Standards for Barrier Free Built Environment for Persons With Disabilities **and Elderly Persons** (MoUD, March 2016);
  - (c)(i) "website standard as specified in the guidelines for Indian Government websites, as adopted by" DARPG;
  - (c)(ii) documents on websites to be in ePUB or OCR-based PDF.
  - Rule 15(2): Ministries and Departments ensure compliance "through the concerned domain regulators or otherwise".
  - [SC judgment para 60, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **ICT standard added:** IS 17802 (Part 1) 2021 and IS 17802 (Part 2) 2022 (BIS) became item (iii) in Rule 15(1)(c) through the RPwD (Amendment) Rules 2023, notified 10.05.2023. — [SC judgment para 63 and footnote 61, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Health-specific standard added:** "Accessibility Standards for Healthcare, notified by the Government of India in the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare dated 4 May 2023" became clause (g) of Rule 15(1) through the RPwD (Amendment) Rules 2023, notified 09.08.2023. — [SC judgment para 63 and footnote 65, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
  - Other sources give the notification date as 10 Aug 2023 or 1 June 2023. — [Lexplosion, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://lexplosion.in/rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-rules-gets-amended-to-include-accessibility-standards-for-health-care/)
  - One summary says the healthcare standards prescribe compliance by "Government and private hospitals, and other health care institutions and centres". — [same Lexplosion source, snippet](https://lexplosion.in/rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-rules-gets-amended-to-include-accessibility-standards-for-health-care/)
  - The MoHFW standards PDF returned 403 on 2026-11-15. — [MoHFW PDF](https://mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/Accessibility_Standard_English_merged.pdf)
- **Count of standards:** By November 2024, Rule 15(1) listed 17 documents in clauses (a) to (p). — [SC judgment para 64, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)

#### 1C. Rajive Raturi v. Union of India (Supreme Court, 8 Nov 2024)
- **Bench:** Dr D.Y. Chandrachud CJI, J.B. Pardiwala J and Manoj Misra J. Writ petition instituted in 2005. — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Holding (paras 74-75):** "Rule 15, in its current form, does not provide for non-negotiable compulsory standards, but only persuasive guidelines." "Several of the guidelines prescribed in Rule 15, appear to be recommendatory guidelines, under the garb of mandatory rules. Rule 15(1) is thus ultra vires." — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Direction (para 76):** The Union must "delineate mandatory rules, as required by Section 40, within a period of three months", in consultation with stakeholders and with NALSAR-CDS involved. Progressive compliance with the existing Rule 15(1) "must continue unabated". — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Enforcement (para 77):** Once the rules are prescribed, the Union, States and UTs must apply the consequences in Sections 44, 45, 46 and 89, "including the holding back of completion certificates and imposition of fines". — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Principles for the new rules (para 78):** These include "Assistive Technology Integration: Mandating the integration of assistive and adaptive technologies, such as screen readers, audio descriptions, and accessible digital interfaces ... across public and private platforms". — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Next steps (paras 80-81):** The Court directed MoSJE to pay NALSAR-CDS ₹50,00,000 by 15 Dec 2024 and adjourned the matter to 7 March 2025 for a compliance report. — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Elderly mention (para 45):** The NALSAR-CDS report noted that the 2021 Harmonised Guidelines broadened accessibility needs to "groups beyond individuals with disabilities, such as children, the elderly, and those temporarily disabled". — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Health evidence cited by the Court (para 48(g)):** "A Survey by National Centre for Promotion of Employment for Disabled People found that healthcare infrastructure is frequently not designed with accessibility in mind." — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)
- **Digital evidence cited by the Court (para 48(h)):** "Only 25% of sports websites were fully accessible, and merely 7.1% of apps met accessibility standards." This is the only quantified digital audit in the judgment, and it is about sports, not health. — [SC judgment, read](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2005/9321/9321_2005_1_1503_56986_Judgement_08-Nov-2024.pdf)

#### 1D. Rules after Raturi, 2025-2026 (status at 2026-11-15: drafts, none confirmed as final)
- **Missed deadline and slow drafting:** The three-month deadline was missed. Six groups drafted rules, published "starting last July" (i.e., July 2025). One draft is named: "Draft Non-Negotiable Rules for Products for Persons with Disabilities".
  - At the 11 Feb 2026 hearing, petitioners argued that withholding completion certificates offers no deterrent for websites, because they need no prior clearance. They also argued that DEPwD alone carries the compliance burden.
  - The Court gave the Centre four more months.
  - [ThePrint, 20 Feb 2026, fetched](https://theprint.in/judiciary/has-landmark-sc-order-on-accessibility-for-disabled-translated-on-ground-compliance-hearing-bares-gaps/2859044)
- **Draft RPwD (Amendment) Rules 2026 (ICT):**
  - Instrument: S.O. 3962(E), dated 16 July 2026, published in the Gazette on 20 July 2026, issued by MoSJE (DEPwD). It revises Rule 15(1)(c).
  - Standard: IS 17802 Parts 1 and 2.
  - Scope: websites, mobile and tablet apps, documents, ICT-based public facilities and services, and ICT hardware. It covers establishments "located within India or outside India".
  - Deadlines: one year from publication for establishments with turnover of ₹500 crore or more, 18 months for others, and full compliance within two years (Section 46). Government procurement policies must be revised within 90 days.
  - Accessibility Conformance Report (ACR): mandatory, published free, re-tested after major changes, reviewed at least every two years, and signed by a nodal officer of at least Director rank.
  - Penalties: Section 89 fines imposed by the Chief or State Commissioner, possible suspension or cancellation of registrations, and enhanced fines if a lapse is not fixed within 90 days of an order.
  - Comments: due 30 days from 20 July 2026.
  - The article does not specifically mention healthcare.
  - [IndiaLaw LLP blog, accessed 2026-11-15, fetched](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/regulatory/rpwd-amendment-rules-2026/); [ThePrint, 27 Jul 2026, fetched](https://theprint.in/tech/global-tech-firms-to-come-under-indias-mandatory-disability-access-rules-face-fines-licence-risk/2997906/)
- **Supreme Court order of 29 July 2026** (Pardiwala and K.V. Viswanathan JJ):
  - The rules were "not yet finalised or published in the gazette".
  - The Centre must consider experts' and litigants' suggestions before notifying them.
  - All states must appoint State Commissioners under Section 79 within four weeks.
  - Compliance affidavits are due, and the next hearing is "January next year" (i.e., January 2027).
  - [Verdictum, 29 Jul 2026, fetched](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/expert-inputs-for-robust-lacunae-free-accessibility-rules-under-rpwd-act-1618751)
- **Draft built-environment rules (2025):** A draft RPwD (Amendment) Rules on "non-negotiable accessibility standards in buildings and built environment", 2025, was released for comment. Sources conflict on the dates (a comment deadline of 20 June 2025 versus Gazette publication on 12 Dec 2025). — [Lexplosion, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://lexplosion.in/comments-invited-on-draft-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-amendment-rules-on-non-negotiable-accessibility-standards-in-buildings-and-built-environment-2025/)

#### 1E. GIGW 3.0 and its WCAG basis
- **Title and certification:** The official portal is titled "Guidelines for Indian Government Websites and apps (GIGW 3.0)". It is developed and hosted by NIC (MeitY). "STQC offers Certified Quality Website (CQW) certification for GIGW compliant websites", and the portal links a list of compliant websites and a "GIGW Compliance & Certification Handbook". — [guidelines.india.gov.in, accessed 2026-11-15, fetched](https://guidelines.india.gov.in/)
- **WCAG basis:** GIGW 3.0 "ensures conformity with Level AA of WCAG 2.1", adding 17 new success criteria over GIGW 2.0, which was based on WCAG 2.0. Its CERT-In cybersecurity chapter covers "websites, web portals, web applications and mobile apps", and government organisations need a "safe to host" certificate. — [guidelines.india.gov.in/?p=10063, page last updated 13.11.2026, fetched](https://guidelines.india.gov.in/?p=10063)
- **Scope:** "Government organisations are expected to carefully assess their existing websites, web portals, web applications and mobile apps against GIGW 3.0." — [guidelines.india.gov.in focus areas, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://guidelines.india.gov.in/focus-areas/)
- **Launch date:** MeitY launched GIGW 3.0 by PIB release dated 7 March 2023, per a secondary reference list; I did not verify the PIB page. — [IMPRI, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/digital-governance-gigw-3-0/)
- **Weak enforcement (pre-3.0 data):**
  - The STQC directorate had certified only 285 central and state websites (MeitY Annual Report 2021-22), against 4,142 Union government websites listed on the National Portal.
  - Many portals, including **Ayushman Bharat**, claimed GIGW compliance while stating only WCAG Level A, although GIGW requires Level AA.
  - [Scroll.in, 4 Nov 2022, fetched](https://scroll.in/article/1036006/few-indian-websites-are-technically-accessible-to-disabled-people-despite-the-law-requiring-this)
- **Private health platforms (CCPD view):** In the Practo case the Chief Commissioner held that "Sections 40 and 42 of the RPwD Act, 2016, together with IS 17802 of BIS and Rule 15(1) of the RPwD Rules, 2017, are mandatory and self-effectuating" for a private online health platform. This is a quasi-judicial view expressed after Raturi struck down Rule 15(1). — [CCPD order, Case No. 13205/1102/2023, hearing 22.07.2025, signed 7 Aug 2025, read](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s39fb7b048c96d44a0337f049e0a61ff06/uploads/2025/08/202508081355021472.pdf)

#### 1G. MWPSC Act 2007, Section 20 (medical support for senior citizens)
- **Text:** The State Government shall ensure that:
  - government hospitals, or hospitals funded fully or partially by the government, provide beds for all senior citizens "as far as possible";
  - "separate queues be arranged for senior citizens";
  - facilities for treatment of chronic, terminal and degenerative diseases are expanded for senior citizens;
  - research into chronic elderly diseases and ageing is expanded;
  - "there are earmarked facilities for geriatric patients in every district hospital" headed by a medical officer with geriatric-care experience.
  - [AdvocateKhoj bare act, Section 20, accessed 2026-11-15, fetched](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/maintenance/20.php)
- **Scope:** The duty falls on State Governments and covers only government and government-funded hospitals. Private hospitals are outside Section 20 as enacted. — [same, fetched](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/maintenance/20.php)
- **2019 Amendment Bill:**
  - Introduced in the Lok Sabha on 11 Dec 2019. The Standing Committee reported on 29 Jan 2021.
  - It would have extended separate queues and beds "to all hospitals, including private ones".
  - It would have required care homes run by government or private organisations to register with a state authority, with central minimum standards on infrastructure and medical facilities, and homecare agencies to employ trained, certified staff and register.
  - [PRS Legislative Research bill page, accessed 2026-11-15, fetched](https://www.prsindia.org/billtrack/maintenance-and-welfare-parents-and-senior-citizens-amendment-bill-2019)
- **Status:** "The Bill lapsed after dissolution of Lok Sabha", per the MoSJE written reply (Minister B.L. Verma) in the Winter Session, December 2025. The minister said a new policy is needed. No replacement bill was reported. — [Outlook Money, 4 Dec 2025, fetched](https://www.outlookmoney.com/retirement/senior-living/maintenance-and-welfare-of-parents-and-senior-citizens-act-are-amendments-in-the-pipeline)
- **State implementation evidence (court-driven and fragmentary):**
  - Madras High Court (K. Arumugam, 2016): guidelines for separate outpatient queues for OP chits, check-ups, labs and drug collection in Tamil Nadu government hospitals, in a petition seeking special geriatric wards in district hospitals under Section 20. — [LiveLaw, 2016, snippet](https://livelaw.in/senior-citizens-will-get-special-treatment-government-hospitals-assures-madras-hc)
  - Karnataka High Court (COVID era): the state "shall make every possible endeavour to comply with the mandate of Section 20", including directions to COVID hospitals. — [Medical Dialogues, snippet, accessed 2026-11-15](https://medicaldialogues.in/state-news/karnataka/make-comprehensive-plans-to-ensure-medical-facilities-to-senior-citizens-karnataka-high-court-to-state-77031)

- **Size of the standard:** 639 objective elements (OEs), 105 of them "Core". — [NABH 6th Edition, Jan 2025, Foreword, read](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Old age alone does not make a patient a "person with disability" in any source reviewed. Older adults fall under the RPwD regime only when an impairment qualifies. In practice, the elderly benefit indirectly: Rule 15(1)(a)'s incorporated building standard is explicitly for "Persons with Disabilities and Elderly Persons", and NABH treats the elderly as "vulnerable patients" (COP.16).
- For digital access, the binding hooks are:
  - Section 46, which covers government and private service providers alike;
  - Rule 15(1)(c), which incorporates GIGW and IS 17802 for "every establishment";
  - GIGW 3.0 itself, which binds government organisations, so it covers NHA (ABHA), MoHFW (eSanjeevani) and NIC/MoHFW (ORS), but not private hospitals.

  Raturi left Rule 15(1) ultra vires and the replacement ICT rule is still a draft (July 2026). So in November 2026, enforcement against private hospital websites rests on CCPD complaint-by-complaint orders (Practo, August 2025) rather than on a settled, notified mandatory standard.
- Section 20 of the MWPSC Act places no obligation on private hospitals. The 2019 bill that would have extended it lapsed, so private-sector queue, bed and geriatric obligations in the reviewed material arise only from NABH (voluntary) and from PM-JAY empanelment conditions.
- NABH's patient-communication requirements (language "they can understand", "understandable" discharge instructions, complaint mechanisms) are the most concrete obligations relevant to older patients. They apply only to accredited facilities: roughly 2,500 fully accredited and 12,500 entry-level facilities in October 2024, against an estimated 80,000 hospitals. The quality incentives are percentage uplifts on PM-JAY or insurer package rates.

## Key Question 2: What are the exact budget figures and dates, and which body controls each?

### Takeaway
The figures below come from the Union **Notes on Demands for Grants 2026-27**, which I read directly. Each line gives Actual 2024-25 / BE 2025-26 / RE 2025-26 / BE 2026-27.

- **AVYAY centrally sponsored component** (IPSrC and SAPSrC): ₹146.13 cr / ₹263.73 cr / ₹230 cr / ₹355 cr. Both AVYAY lines are met entirely from the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund.

Who controls each budget:
- PM-JAY money is controlled by the NHA (Governing Board chaired by the Union Health Minister) and spent through State Health Agencies on a 60:40 Centre-State split.
- AVYAY money is controlled by MoSJE and released to NGOs, State Governments and implementing agencies.
- Inside government hospitals, the Rogi Kalyan Samiti's Member Secretary (the Medical Superintendent) holds all executive and financial powers. Proposals above ₹10 lakh at a district hospital need Governing Body approval.

CSR on "senior citizens welfare" was ₹159.82 crore in FY 2023-24, about 0.46% of total CSR of ₹34,908.75 crore.

#### 2E. Hospital-level control of patient-facing spending
- **Government hospitals: Rogi Kalyan Samiti (RKS).** Source: MoHFW Guidelines for Rogi Kalyan Samitis in Public Health Facilities, issued 19 June 2015 (NHSRC). [Read](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/13.NUHM%20%28NHM%29%20RKS%20Guidelines.pdf)
  - **Legal form:** A registered society under the Societies Registration Act 1860, at district hospitals, CHCs and PHCs.
  - **Governing Body (GB) at a district hospital:** Chaired by the "In-charge Minister/local MP/President Zila Panchayat/District Magistrate". The Member Secretary is the "Medical Superintendent/Civil Surgeon/Hospital-in-charge".
  - **Financial authority:** "All executive and financial powers of the society shall vest in the Member Secretary."
  - **Executive Committee (EC) at a district hospital:** Chaired by the District Magistrate, with the Civil Surgeon/Hospital-in-charge as Member Secretary.
  - **Approval thresholds:** The GB approves financial proposals "beyond the powers of the Executive Committee; i.e. over Rs. 10 lakhs at the level of the DH, Rs. 7 lakhs at the CHC, and Rs. 2 lakhs at the PHC".
  - **Sources of funds:**
    - untied funds "under NHM by State Health Society/District Health Society based on the level of facility, its case load, fund utilization capacity and availability of previous year funds";
    - user fees set by the GB and implemented by the EC;
    - donations and government grants;
    - leasing of premises.
  - **Patient-facing duties:** The EC reviews "compliance to the Patient's Charter" and runs the grievance redressal mechanism. RKS should set up a grievance redressal desk.
- **Earlier corpus grant (NRHM):** ₹5,00,000 corpus grant per district hospital RKS (Lok Sabha reply, 2013, dated). — [eparlib, 2013, snippet](https://eparlib.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/645473/1/144809.pdf)
- **Who controls PM-JAY money:** State Health Agencies settle claims to hospitals; see 2A. — [ThePrint, 29 Jul 2025, fetched](https://theprint.in/india/over-1-06-lakh-claims-settled-under-ayushman-vay-vandana-scheme-rajya-sabha-told/2706708)
- **Private hospitals: NABH governance model (6th ed).** [Read](https://portal.nabh.co/images/Standards/NABH%20Hospital%20Accreditation%20Standard%206th%20Edition%20January%202025.pdf)
  - **"Responsible for governance":** owners, partners, trustees or the board of directors, or "in the case of public hospitals, the respective Ministry".
  - **"Leadership":** "CEO, COO, Managing Director, Dean / Director, Medical Director / Medical Superintendent".
  - **ROM.1.c:** Those responsible for governance "approve the documented strategic and operational plans and the organisation's annual budget".
  - **ROM.4:** The organisation is headed by a leader responsible for day-to-day operation and for compliance with "applicable legislations".
  - **ROM.5.c:** "The organisation plans and budgets for its activities annually."
  - **PSQ.2.e:** "There is a designated individual for coordinating and implementing the quality improvement programme."
  - **PSQ.6.d:** "Organisation earmarks adequate funds from its annual budget" for patient safety and quality improvement.

### Inferences
- PM-JAY's BE 2026-27 (₹9,500 crore) is only ₹94 crore above BE 2025-26 (₹9,406 crore). RE 2025-26 fell to ₹9,000 crore, even though Vay Vandana admissions passed ₹5,668.80 crore in two years and the 70+ group adds about 6 crore people. The 70+ cost therefore appears to be absorbed within an almost flat central PM-JAY line, with states paying 40%. This is my calculation from the cited figures; there is no official 70+ budget line.
- Average authorised value per Vay Vandana admission is about ₹20,856 as of Dec 2025 (₹2,154.37 crore over 10.33 lakh) and about ₹24,164 as of Oct 2026 (₹5,668.80 crore over 23.46 lakh). Cataract and dialysis dominate by count. These are my calculations.
- **Scale of elder-specific lines:** The two AVYAY lines total ₹740 crore in BE 2026-27, all drawn from the Senior Citizens' Welfare Fund rather than the Consolidated Fund. The NPHCE amount is hidden inside a ₹490 crore Tertiary Care line shared with about seven other programmes. Elder-specific health spending outside PM-JAY is small and hard to trace.
- **CSR:** Elder care gets about 0.46% of CSR (₹159.82 crore in FY 2023-24). That is far below CSR "health care" (₹7,150.81 crore) and below the two AVYAY lines combined (BE 2025-26 ₹553.42 crore). The funding that does reach elder health (for example HelpAge MHUs) is mostly mobile primary care.
- **Who signs off on patient-facing spending:**
  - In public hospitals, the Medical Superintendent, as RKS Member Secretary, holds day-to-day executive and financial powers. Larger items (above ₹10 lakh at a district hospital) need the GB, which is chaired by a politician or the DM. NHM untied funds arrive through the State or District Health Society.
  - In NABH-accredited private hospitals, the board or owners approve the annual budget, and the CEO/MS runs operations with a designated quality coordinator.
  - These are the documented control points. No empirical study of actual budget control was found.

## Key Question 3: Where is data absent? (consolidated NOT FOUND register)

### Takeaway
The biggest blind spots:
- **Compliance evidence:** No published accessibility audits of national digital health platforms (ABHA, eSanjeevani, ORS, PM-JAY portals) or of Indian hospital websites, and no outcome for the CoWIN audit the Supreme Court ordered in 2021.
- **Budget transparency:** No NPHCE line item, no separate 70+ PM-JAY cost line, and no FY 2024-25 elder-care CSR figure.
- **MWPSC Section 20:** No state-level implementation data.

### Cited Findings
- The SC-ordered disability audit of CoWIN and Aarogya Setu (31 May 2021): outcome not reported in later reviews (2024). — [The News Minute, 3 Jun 2021](https://thenewsminute.com/amp/story/news/cowin-not-conducive-persons-disabilities-sc-union-govt-150004); [Newslaundry, 6 Sep 2024](https://www.newslaundry.com/2024/09/06/why-does-india-fail-to-address-digital-accessibility-gaps)

### Inferences
- Regulators have legal hooks: Sections 40, 42 and 46, Rule 15 with IS 17802, GIGW 3.0, and Section 20 of the MWPSC Act. Even so, no body routinely publishes compliance data for health websites or for elder-specific hospital facilities. Evidence of non-compliance surfaces only through individual litigation: the SC COVID case, the CCPD Practo case and the CCPD notices to Delhi hospitals.
- Money flows are traceable at scheme level: PM-JAY, AVYAY and NHM. They are not traceable at the level of elder-specific health services, such as NPHCE geriatric units or Section 20 queues and beds, or at hospital level, such as RKS spending on elder-friendly services.
