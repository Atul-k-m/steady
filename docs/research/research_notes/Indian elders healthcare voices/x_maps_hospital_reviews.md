# Indian elders and healthcare: voices on X/Twitter, Google Maps reviews and hospital review sites

## 1. Header

**Scope.** Public posts and reviews in which Indian adults aged 60+, or the family members helping them, describe problems with healthcare. Problems and evidence only. IDs use the prefix `SOC-`. Collected on 2026-11-15.

**Result in one line.** There are 21 dated items, and all 21 have verbatim quotes. The sources are X/Twitter (4, all seen through news articles that quote the posts, because X itself is login-walled), Practo (9), Google Maps reviews (1, through Google's own Business Profile mirror), Trustpilot (6) and PissedConsumer (1). The item says the patient is 60+ in **9 of 21** (SOC-04, 05, 09, 10, 11, 13, 16, 17, 18). In the other 12 the reviewer writes about "my father/mother/dad/mum" without giving an age. Each such row says "age not stated".

**How quotes were captured (read this before reusing them).**
- `page` means the text came from a fetched page. For Practo, Trustpilot, PissedConsumer and the news articles, the page was read through a fetch tool that returns excerpts of at most 125 characters each. So the quotes are verbatim *excerpts*, not full reviews. A model makes that extraction, and there is a small risk of transcription slips. Re-check against the URL before publishing.
- SOC-19 (Google review) was downloaded as raw HTML and is exact character-for-character.
- `snippet-only` means the text was seen only in a search-engine summary. Those summaries are produced by a model and may not be raw snippets, so treat anything marked `snippet-only` as unverified.
- Practo and Google show **relative dates** only, such as "3 years ago". These were converted using the fetch date 2026-11-15 and are marked "≈".

**Date range of items.** ≈2020 to 2026-10. 20 of the 21 items fall in 2021–2026. SOC-14 is ≈2020.

**Short answers to the key questions (details in the table and section 3).**
- *What families raise when they tag or post.* Queueing for months for surgery at AIIMS (SOC-01/02). Emergency care withdrawn once a patient is classed as a free-quota (EWS) case (SOC-03). No wheelchair at a government hospital (SOC-04). After a routine check-up registration, the family's phone number reached pharmacies and labs (SOC-07). Online pharmacies and home sample collection going wrong for a parent (SOC-08, 20, 21).
- *Google Maps.* Only private-hospital mirrors were reachable, and they show ~10 mostly positive "most relevant" reviews. One review describes a previous hospital where "basic tests seemed conditional on immediate payments" (SOC-19). Reviews of the government hospitals named in the brief: NOT FOUND (see section 4).
- *Practo.* This was the richest source for S5. Problems include: long waits despite a booking (SOC-09, 11, 16, 17), consultations of a few minutes (SOC-05, 16), the doctor not explaining (SOC-16), disregarding existing reports or CT scans (SOC-11, 18), pushing surgery on 70- and 84-year-olds (SOC-10, 16), brushing off the parent's own prescription (SOC-14), and refusing post-op time (SOC-15).
- *Who does the task.* Almost always an adult child: son, daughter, or the daughter's husband in SOC-12. The one clear case of an elder acting alone is SOC-11, where a 72+ father "called in 15 times" to get an appointment. In SOC-10 the caregiver is also looking after a second, ailing parent.

## 3. Workarounds observed

- **Going public on X to escalate** (SOC-01, SOC-03). In SOC-01 the son posted from the queue. AIIMS replied publicly and said it had phoned the family "on the phone number obtained from hospital records" (reply text quoted in the [Free Press Journal article](https://www.freepressjournal.in/india/currently-doesnt-need-any-help-aiims-delhi-responds-after-sons-emotional-post-over-procedural-delay-on-fathers-heart-surgery)). The two sides then disputed the facts. A search summary of the coverage says AIIMS stated the patient was at home in his village, while the son said "I am in Delhi, and not Deoria." (snippet-only, via [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/troubled-by-queues-at-aiims-son-turns-to-social-media/story)). Escalation got a response but did not, on the record seen, remove the underlying wait. SOC-03: the thread drew public attention; no outcome is stated for the patient.
- **Offering staff money for a wheelchair, then carrying the parent** (SOC-04). The son said "I agreed to pay. But then she asked me to wait for 30 more minutes." It failed because paying did not bring the wheelchair quickly, so he carried his father himself: "I managed to carry my father to the ground floor using the elevator." Search summaries of the coverage say two contract supervisors were suspended afterwards (snippet-only).
- **Booking through an app to avoid the wait** (SOC-09). The family booked through Practo so the 88-year-old would not wait. It failed because the clinic did not honour the slot: "we are really disappointed, the way the medical center handling practo booking." (same review).
- **The elder persisting on the phone** (SOC-11). The father "called in 15 times to get an appointment". The appointment was got, but it still meant a wait and a consultation that ignored his reports.
- **Seeking a second opinion** (SOC-10, SOC-18; also SOC-19). In SOC-18 the second doctor was worse ("didn't even bother to see the CT scan report"). In SOC-10 the family resisted surgery pressure, held back by fear about "an operation at this age". In SOC-19 the family moved the father ICU-to-ICU to another hospital on a doctor's recommendation, and it worked: "I only wish we had come to Amrita sooner."
- **Remote or nearer follow-up to save the parent travel** (SOC-13). The doctor arranged things "so that my mother wouldn’t have to keep travelling all the way to Delhi." The review presents this as a success.
- **Ordering medicines, devices or home lab collection online for a parent** (SOC-08, SOC-20, SOC-21). This is used to avoid travel. It failed through a wrong product and delay (SOC-08), aggressive 5:15 AM calls and an overloaded phlebotomist (SOC-20), and medicines put on hold with repeated calls from people "POSING AS MBBS DOC" (SOC-21).
- **Giving the family's phone number at registration** (SOC-07). This is a routine part of registration, not a chosen workaround. It backfired: "Over 70+ calls from random pharmacies and diagnostic companies in just one day!"
