# App store reviews: older Indians (60+) and family caregivers struggling with healthcare tasks in Indian health apps

## 1. Header

**Scope.** Verbatim, dated Google Play and Apple App Store (India storefront) reviews of Indian health apps in which the reviewer mentions a parent, grandparent, in-law, elderly person, senior citizen, pensioner or retired person, or a 60+/70+ age, and describes a problem with a healthcare task: finding a doctor, booking, OTP/login, registration, teleconsult, tests and reports, medicines, refunds, insurance/Ayushman/CGHS, or a digital health ID. This file records problems and evidence only. It makes no product or feature recommendations. Where a reviewer asks for a feature, the request is quoted as evidence of a pain, not endorsed.

**Method.** I pulled Play reviews with the `google-play-scraper` Python package (country='in'): up to 2,600 newest English reviews, 1,000 "most relevant" English reviews and up to 1,000 newest reviews with lang='hi' per app, de-duplicated by review ID. App Store reviews came from the public iTunes RSS feed `https://itunes.apple.com/in/rss/customerreviews/page=N/id={ID}/sortby=mostrecent/json`, pages 1–10, which caps out at 500 reviews per app. A regex keyword filter (below) selected 582 reviews, and I read all of them by hand. 141 became items. Each quote was copied programmatically from the API/feed JSON and checked by script: every excerpt segment is an exact substring of the source text, in order. No reviewer names, usernames, avatars or profile links were kept. The scraper fields for those were deleted at download time.

**Quote conventions.**
- `[...]` marks an omission I made. A bare `...` or `....` inside a quote is the reviewer's own punctuation.
- `<br>` marks a line break in the original.
- `\|` is an escaped pipe character.
- App Store review titles are shown as `[Review title: ...]`.
- Spelling, grammar, Hindi/Hinglish, Telugu script and emojis are kept as written.
- Glosses (English renderings) are mine and appear only in the Gloss column.
- I trimmed some long quotes to leave out the names of staff, doctors, patients and delivery agents. For the same reason, one App Store title that names a staff member (APP-41) is replaced with `[Review title omitted: it names a staff member]`.

**Date range of items.** 2020-11-13 to 2026-11-14. 140 of the 141 items are dated 2021 or later: 2021 (10), 2022 (5), 2023 (5), 2024 (11), 2025 (23), 2026 (86). One is from 2020 (APP-29), kept because it is the clearest case of an elder ordering alone and being shut out by chat-only support. Every item has a date, so there is **no Undated list (none)**.

**Skew to note.** For high-volume apps, the Play "newest" sort covers only the last weeks or months (1mg: about 1 month), so the commercial pharmacy items are mostly 2026. Older items come from the "most relevant" sort. Ayushman 70+ (Ayushman Vay Vandana) enrolment complaints cluster from Oct 2024 onward. myCGHS complaints cluster around the May 2025 switch to myCGHS 2.0.

**Caveat on age.** "Mother/father/parents" does not prove the person is 60+. Explicit ages of 60 or over, or the words senior citizen/elderly/pensioner/old age/70+, appear in about half the items (for example APP-02, APP-16, APP-19, APP-23, APP-37, APP-38, APP-42, APP-43, APP-53, APP-83–APP-87, and most of APP-98–APP-120). The rest are parents or grandparents of unstated age.

## 3. Workarounds observed

Short phrases in quotation marks are verbatim from the cited review.

**a. Buying from a local chemist, or searching the town**
- Seen in: APP-31 ("finally searched the city and got it"), APP-32 ("just go to near well ness medical and purchased medicine from it"), APP-33 ("should have called the local shop and sent the medicine", in hindsight), APP-35 ("urging everyone to just go to the local shop to buy meds"), APP-20 ("buy from a trusted local pharmacy instead"), APP-51 ("majboori me bahar se dwai magani pdi", i.e. forced to buy outside), APP-56 (would rather have gone to the medical store himself), APP-133 ("I have bought another item from my nearest medical store").
- Why it fails or is not available:
  - The medicine is not stocked locally, which is often the reason for ordering online in the first place: APP-17 (asthma medicine not available in the village), APP-21 ("these medicines are not readily available at my place"), APP-34 ("not available in any nearby stores"), APP-130 (medicines "not available in locally and our doctor is 50- 60 km from our location").
  - A last-minute cancellation removes the fallback after the window has passed: APP-24 ("we would have gone to the pharmacy and purchased them ourselves").
  - The caregiver lives in another city and cannot go: APP-38, APP-50.

**b. Calling customer care, helpline or chat, and escalating**
- This is the most common workaround and the most common failure.
- Ways it fails:
  - IVR loops, and phone lines that serve only new bookings: APP-23 ("their IVR traps you on endless hold music").
  - Agents available only by day: APP-13.
  - Bots or AI that never reach a human: APP-126 ("never connects to a human executive").
  - No option to call at all: APP-21.
  - Repeated "today" or "by this evening" promises: APP-17, APP-56.
  - Being told to cancel and reorder, or to buy elsewhere: APP-20, APP-134 ("customer care ex mentions to cancel and place the order else"), APP-139.
  - Escalations that change nothing: APP-19 ("completely helpless"), APP-44 (5 calls), APP-137.
  - Complaints auto-cancelled: APP-26.
  - Helplines that say nothing can be done or that disconnect: APP-78 ("nothing can be done now"), APP-94 ("Help line toll free number also disconnecting").
  - Calls redirected endlessly: APP-96.
  - Grievances closed as "resolved" without resolution: APP-103.
  - Chat-only support that elderly parents could not use: APP-29 ("requested a replacement over chat (which my parents could not do)").

**c. Going in person (hospital, government office, service centre, another doctor or lab)**
