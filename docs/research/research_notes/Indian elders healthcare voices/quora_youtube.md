# Indian elders (60+) and their families on healthcare processes: verbatim YouTube comments and Quora

## 1. Header

**Scope.** Adults aged 60+ in India, and the family members who help them, dealing with healthcare from finding a doctor to medicines and follow-up. The sources are (a) YouTube comments under Hindi/Hinglish how-to tutorials and news videos, and (b) Quora questions and answers. The file records problems and evidence only. It contains no product, app or feature suggestions, and hand-tremor or pointer topics were not searched.

**Collection date:** 2026-11-15 (local) / 2026-11-16 (UTC).

**Method (YouTube).** I ran `yt-dlp 2026.10.26` in a scratchpad venv with `--skip-download --write-comments --extractor-args "youtube:max_comments=300,all,100"`, then read the `comments` array in each `.info.json`. I filtered comments by keyword (family and elder terms, plus problem terms such as OTP, slot, reject, KYC, CSC, dalal and line), read them by hand, and re-checked each selected quote against the raw JSON by comment ID. Videos were found with yt-dlp `ytsearch` queries, because web search does not return YouTube results well.

**Quote conventions.**
- Quotes are copied character-for-character from the yt-dlp JSON `text` field. A line break in the original is shown as ` / `. Double spaces are kept as they appear in the original.
- My omissions are marked ` ... ` (with a space on both sides). Any other `...`, `..` or `....` inside a quote is the commenter's own.
- Commenter names, handles and profile links are not recorded. Where a comment began with an @handle or contained a person's name, that part is omitted with ` ... `.

**Date precision (important).** YouTube shows only relative ages ("1 year ago"). yt-dlp turns these into a timestamp relative to the fetch date, so every YouTube date below is approximate. The bracket gives the original label:
- `[3w]`, `[1m]` … `[11m]`: weeks or months ago. Roughly ±1 month.
- `[1y]` = "1 year ago": the true date falls between 2024-11-17 and 2025-11-16. It is shown as ~2025-11-16.
- `[2y]`: between 2023-11-17 and 2024-11-16. `[3y]`: between 2022-11-17 and 2023-11-16. `[4y]`: between 2021-11-17 and 2022-11-16.

## 3. Workarounds observed

**Child, grandchild or friend does the online task for the elder.** This is the dominant pattern: QYT-04, 05, 07, 10, 27–31, 33–35, 37, 38, 41–43, 48, 49, 66–78. It fails when:
- the system binds identity to whoever's phone or details were used. The slip carries the daughter's details (QYT-04); the son is recorded as the mother's spouse (QYT-27).
- the elder must be physically present for a live photo or biometric (QYT-38, 40).
- the helper cannot leave the elder to make the office visits the process needs (QYT-69).

**Using a relative's or someone else's mobile number for OTP or login.** QYT-04, 27 and 75 use or ask about this. QYT-08 is the reverse case: the OTP goes to a number the user no longer has. QYT-32 asks about seniors whose Aadhaar has no linked phone at all. Why it fails: identity mismatch (QYT-04, 27). For QYT-75 the outcome is unknown.

**CSC or cyber café.** QYT-43, 44, 45, and possibly QYT-41.
- QYT-43: the operator made the grandmother's card but skipped adding family members, so the grandfather cannot be added later.
- QYT-44: CSCs charge ₹150–200 per registration.
- QYT-45: claims cyber cafés charge ₹10,000.
- QYT-41: ₹35,000 demanded to make a 75-year-old's Aadhaar (who demanded it is not stated).

**ASHA worker enrolling door to door.** QYT-46. It is hampered because the interface is English-only.

**Touts ("dalal").** QYT-17: a commenter asks for a dalal's number. No outcome is reported.

**Queueing overnight or before dawn for an OPD card.** QYT-12 (3–4 am), QYT-13 (from 7 pm, over 9 years), QYT-11 (the guard's "write your name in the morning, come back at 9 pm"), QYT-18 (a night outside AIIMS). Why it fails or hurts: it is exhausting and cold, and sometimes ends without a number (QYT-13). Online slots are an alternative, but they are always "full" or "not opened yet" (QYT-05, 06), and follow-ups are not online at all (QYT-22).

**Splitting attendants across two queues.** QYT-14 advises two people, because staff "don't listen" and anyone pushed out of a line restarts at the back.

**Proxy follow-up without the patient.** QYT-20 asks about sending a relative with the reports and a video call. QYT-21 advises claiming the patient is present; the stated risk is that doctors shout and the proxy may not know the history.

**Calling the helpline (14555 for PM-JAY; CGHS numbers).**
- QYT-28: does not connect.
- QYT-54: disconnects after 2 minutes; no grievance can be lodged.
- QYT-48: told "server side problem, try again".
- QYT-64: the helpline said the hospital accepts the card; the hospital refused.
- QYT-70: seven CGHS numbers; wrong number, transferred, retired.
- QYT-69: "no response".

**Waiting it out.** QYT-49: "wait for a month or so". QYT-26: an 83-year-old has repeated eKYC for 2–3 weeks without success.

**Paying out of pocket or borrowing when the card is refused.** QYT-55 (7 days of tests and medicines), QYT-56 (pay for the MRI or wait 20 days), QYT-57 (₹4 lakh loan), QYT-62, 63, 65. QYT-51 and 58 are refusals with no stated outcome.

**Switching hospitals or institutions.** QYT-24: moving from a private hospital to AIIMS because of costly in-house medicines. QYT-59: going hospital to hospital in Noida, all refusing. QYT-19: turned back from AIIMS on seeing the conditions.
