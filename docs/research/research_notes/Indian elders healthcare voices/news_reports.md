# Indian elders and healthcare: verbatim voices from news and NGO reports

## 1. Header

**Scope.** Adults aged 60+ in India, their family caregivers, and frontline staff, describing problems with healthcare in their own words: finding a doctor, booking, registration and queues, waiting, medicines, follow-up, payments and insurance (CGHS, Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY / Vay Vandana), digital health (CoWIN), emergencies, and elders living alone. Problems only. Solutions proposed in articles are deliberately left out. Hand-tremor and pointer issues are out of scope and were not searched.

**Method and verbatim standard.**
- Most articles were downloaded as raw HTML with `curl` and the paragraphs were extracted with a local script (scratch files in `D:\temp\claude\...\scratchpad\NEWS\`). Quotes marked **page (raw HTML)** are copied character-for-character from that extracted text, including curly quotes and apostrophes.
- ThePrint blocks scripted downloads (Cloudflare). Its quotes came through the WebFetch tool's summariser, which I ran twice and which gave the same text both times. They are marked **page (WebFetch)**. The words are reliable, but quote glyphs (straight vs curly) may not match the original.
- The HelpAge India 2026 report is a PDF. I extracted its text with `pypdf`, joining the PDF's line breaks with single spaces. The mismatched quote marks are exactly as they appear in the PDF.
- "..." marks an omission only. I used it to drop a person's name from inside a quote, as the privacy rule requires, and to shorten. It never joins separate sentences. Speakers are recorded by role, age and place only.
- Where an article gives only indirect speech (common in Hindi papers), the item is kept as a dated item but labelled **indirect speech, not a direct quote**.

**Outlets that yielded items (18):** The Tribune (Chandigarh, Delhi, Haryana, Ludhiana editions), Newslaundry, ThePrint, Scroll, The News Minute, Quartz India, Citizen Matters (Mumbai, Bengaluru), OrissaPOST, Brighter Kashmir, Greater Kashmir, NewsGram (also republished by Medbound Times), Medbound Times, The Patriot, 101Reporters, Free Press Journal (quoting Hindustan Times; also Lokmat Times), Amar Ujala, Aaj Tak, Mangalorean.com, plus the NGO HelpAge India (2026 national report and an impact story).

**Outlets searched with no usable item:** The Hindu, Indian Express, Hindustan Times (reached only second-hand through FPJ and Lokmat), Times of India, BBC Hindi, Dainik Bhaskar, Dainik Jagran, Gaon Connection, Khabar Lahariya, IndiaSpend (one 2026 piece found, quoting experts only), The Wire, Down To Earth, Mint, Agewell Foundation (expert quote only), Kerala Kaumudi (page gone). See section 4.

Hindi: आयुष्मान वय वंदना कार्ड बुजुर्ग परेशान; बुजुर्ग मरीज अस्पताल लाइन पर्ची घंटों खड़े; वय वंदना कार्ड अस्पताल ने इलाज से मना; अंगूठा नहीं लग रहा बुजुर्ग आयुष्मान ई-केवाईसी; बुज़ुर्ग वैक्सीन रजिस्ट्रेशन कोविन; गांव में अकेले रहते बुजुर्ग; QR कोड पर्ची बुजुर्ग; Khabar Lahariya / Bhaskar / BBC Hindi variants.

**Date range of items:** 2018-12-19 to 2026-08-04. 31 of the 35 items fall in 2021–2026. Two older items are kept and flagged (NEWS-02 from 2019, NEWS-24 from 2018).

**Coverage.** Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Chandigarh/Punjab (Mohali, Ludhiana), Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Odisha, Jammu & Kashmir, Maharashtra (Mumbai, Pune, Marathwada), Karnataka (Bengaluru, Yadgir), West Bengal (Purulia), Andhra Pradesh (Anantapur). **Totals:** 35 dated items. 32 contain a verbatim direct quote, NEWS-20's being only two words from a legislator. 3 have indirect speech only (NEWS-19, 22, 35). 18 outlets, including 1 NGO.

## 3. Workarounds observed (and why they failed, where stated)

- **Queue gaming: arrive earlier, or join the night "unofficial slot" list.** NEWS-04: after missing out, she resolved to come back at 5.30 am, and "Someone then suggested that I get my name listed for the queue and sit here". Newslaundry describes unofficial night slots run with security guards. NEWS-03: “forcing us to come as early as 5 am to collect a handwritten token”. NEWS-05: “Some patients even queue up early morning so that they don’t return home without being attended to by doctor.” *Why it fails:* waiting still does not guarantee a slot, and NEWS-04 found appointments already booked at 6.30 am.
- **Staying overnight or for days near the facility.** NEWS-05 and NEWS-06: “even stay here for 2-3 days if we don’t get appointment for the day”. NEWS-10 and NEWS-11 sleep in the AIIMS subway because “the capacity there is full” and “we don’t have money to stay somewhere else”.
- **Booking online instead of queueing.** NEWS-02: an ORS booking still meant a 1.5-hour queue to pay Rs 10. NEWS-03: online CGHS appointments “are either unavailable or not happening”.
- **Repeating visits.** NEWS-12: when medicine is out, “repeat the same token-queue-doctor-queue-medicine-queue process all over again”. NEWS-14: two trips, closed once and out of stock once. NEWS-17: three visits in a week for one referral signature. NEWS-16: told to return in 15 days, and the caregiver asks “How could I again face this agony and trauma?”
- **Asking to leave the public scheme.** NEWS-17: the daughter "had even written to officials requesting permission to opt for private care due to the tedious process" (narration).
- **Phoning hospital after hospital to find one that honours the card.** NEWS-18: 24 calls from 6 am. 10 hospitals denied empanelment, 5–6 were unreachable, and some covered only specific specialities.
- **Paying out of pocket when the card is refused.** NEWS-19: Rs 50,000 (indirect speech; the hospital denies it).
- **Traditional or local healers before a doctor.** NEWS-01: “The hakim gave me oils to massage on my knees but it hasn't helped.” / local doctors' injections, but “it always comes back”.
- **Hiring private transport for follow-up.** NEWS-15: Rs 1,200 per trip, “To take my mother to a doctor means spending an entire day travelling”. The family then stopped visits (missed-care).
- **Buying equipment privately.** NEWS-15: an oxygen cylinder for Rs 25,000 because a concentrator cannot run on erratic power.
- **Private ambulance or app taxi when 108 fails.** NEWS-21: an online search found a private ambulance that took 40+ minutes, had “no paramedic, no physician, not even a stretcher”, and asked Rs 5,500 (cut to Rs 2,500). NEWS-22: an app taxi to Chandigarh after about 4 hours on the floor (narration).
- **Family or volunteers doing the digital step (CoWIN).** NEWS-25 (grandson), NEWS-26 (“The offspring took over to help their tech-challenged parents.”), NEWS-30 (volunteer: “In few cases, I used my contact number so that I don’t disturb the person sleeping at night for a one-time password (OTP)”, and a daughter who failed for a month). *Why it failed:* OTP not arriving, slots gone before forms could be filled, new slots released at 2 am, and a limit of four people per phone number.
- **Peer tips on WhatsApp.** NEWS-27: a WhatsApp group found that selecting the city instead of the area code made the hospital appear.
- **Walking in rather than booking online.** NEWS-28: “less exhausting than trying to book an appointment online”. NEWS-29: some walk-ins were sent back the previous day because the server was down.
- **Relying on neighbours, villagers or paid help when children have migrated.** NEWS-32: “When I am ill, villagers look after me”. NEWS-24: neighbours arrived only at 5 am. NEWS-33: a hired home caregiver, and the doctor quoted says most such services are "not well trained or well managed".
