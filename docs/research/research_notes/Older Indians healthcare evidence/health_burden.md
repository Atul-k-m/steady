## 1. Chronic disease prevalence and multimorbidity (2+ conditions), 60+

### Takeaway
LASI Wave 1's own Executive Summary prints multimorbidity (2+ self-reported diagnosed chronic conditions) at **23%** for adults 60+. The text does **not** print a single national "% of 60+ with at least one chronic condition". The widely quoted "75%" comes from the Health Minister's launch statement (news), and one newspaper printed "55%". Peer-reviewed re-analyses of the same LASI data report multimorbidity at 24.1% or 32.1%, depending on which conditions were counted.

**Peer-reviewed re-analyses of LASI Wave 1, 60+** [peer-reviewed]
- Patel P, Muhammad T, Sahoo H. *BMC Geriatrics* 2023;23:53. Used 11 common chronic conditions, N = 31,464 adults aged 60+. "Prevalence of single morbidity was 30.3%, and multimorbidity was 32.1% among older people in India." Kerala multimorbidity 59.2%. ### Inferences
- The multimorbidity estimate for the same LASI 60+ sample varies (23% LASI-ES, 24.1% Chauhan, 27% Deccan Herald, 32.1% Patel). The most likely reason is the list of conditions counted: Patel used 11 conditions, including thyroid and cholesterol. The official report should be the anchor value, and the alternatives should be shown as a range rather than averaged.
- All these figures are *self-reported diagnosed* conditions. Undiagnosed disease is therefore not captured. For depression, LASI-ES itself shows a tenfold gap between diagnosed (0.8%) and screened (8.3%). Diagnosed prevalence probably understates true burden most in poorer and rural groups, because multimorbidity is reported more by urban, educated and richest groups (36%, 37%, 35%).
- Treatment rates for neuro/psychiatric conditions (41%) and stroke (58%) are much lower than for diabetes (83%). This points to continuity-of-care drop-off in S8/S9 for these conditions.

### Gaps
- A single LASI-printed figure for "% of 60+ with at least one chronic condition" was not found in the Executive Summary. The full India Report PDF (iipsindia.ac.in) was larger than 10 MB and could not be fetched. Its chapter tables may print it.

## 2. Polypharmacy (usually 5+ medicines) among Indian older adults

### Takeaway
No national population-based (LASI) polypharmacy estimate was found. The best pooled Indian figure is **49%** (95% CI 42–56) from a 2021 meta-analysis of mostly hospital-based studies. It defines polypharmacy as 5–9 medicines. The one multi-city *community* study found **33.7%** (5+ daily allopathic medicines).

### Inferences
- Hospital-based pooled estimates (≈half of older patients) are likely higher than the community-dwelling prevalence (one-third in urban communities). Neither represents rural community-dwelling elders.
- Definitions differ: 5–9 (meta-analysis), ≥5 (Khaiser), and ≥5 daily for ≥4 weeks (Das). Excessive polypharmacy is ≥9 in Khaiser but ≥10 in the meta-analysis. These figures should not be pooled or compared directly.
- Polypharmacy and self-medication (19.7%) together point to S8 (dispensing, labels, adherence) and S5 (medication reconciliation at consultation) as high-burden stages, especially combined with the near-vision findings in section 3.

## 3. Vision impairment: blindness, MSVI, cataract, uncorrected refractive error, presbyopia / near-vision impairment

### Takeaway
NBVIS 2015–19 (age **50+**) found blindness at **1.99%**, MSVI at **11.77%** and VI at **26.68%**. Rates rise steeply with age: MSVI is **13.00%** at 60–69, **23.97%** at 70–79 and **34.39%** at 80+. Blindness at 80+ is **11.62%**. Cataract caused **66.2%** of blindness. Untreated cataract (48.01%) and uncorrected refractive error (41.53%) caused most VI. **74.2%** of those examined had uncorrected presbyopia. LASI (measured, **60+**) found 32% with low near vision and 15% with low distance vision.

### Inferences
- Among 60+, MSVI rises from 13.00% (60–69) to 23.97% (70–79) and 34.39% (≥80), and any VI reaches 67.33% at 80+ (NBVIS, 50+ survey). VI is therefore the norm, not the exception, among the oldest patients at S4 (signage, token displays), S7 (reading reports) and S8 (medicine labels).
- Near-vision problems are the largest single visual burden for reading tasks: 74.2% uncorrected presbyopia (50+, NBVIS), 32% low near vision (60+, LASI, *with* available correction) and 51.2% NVI in care homes (60+). - Since cataract causes most blindness and almost half of VI, a large share of the burden is surgically treatable. This connects vision burden to S6/S9 (referral, surgery, follow-up) as well as to reading tasks.

## 4. Hearing loss among older adults (60+)

### Takeaway
Self-report in LASI puts ear/hearing problems at **10%** of adults 60+ (Figure 18: 9.6), and only "around 1%" of 60+ use hearing devices. Measured audiometry in the LASI-DAD subsample (wave 2, 2022–24) printed only counts: 506 with no loss, 1,465 mild and 1,435 moderate, out of 3,406 tested. No percentages were published. NSS 76th round (2018) prints hearing disability at 0.3% for all ages. Its age-wise table was not accessible.

### Inferences
- Self-reported hearing problems (≈10%, LASI 2017-18; 21%, BKPAI seven states) and audiometric loss (the large majority of 3,406 LASI-DAD participants had PTA >25 dB) are far apart. Self-report probably under-captures mild and moderate loss, which still affects S5 conversation, S4 name/token calling and S11 phone/teleconsult.
- Hearing-aid uptake is very low across sources: ~1% of all 60+ (LASI-ES), 7.8% of those with hearing difficulty (Roy & Das), 5% (BKPAI). Most older adults with hearing loss arrive at a consultation unaided.
- The NSS "disability" framing (0.3% all ages) measures a much narrower construct than hearing loss. It should not be compared with the LASI or audiometric figures.

- NOT FOUND: an Indian systematic review or meta-analysis of hearing-loss prevalence in older adults. The only India hearing meta-analysis found covers school children.

## 5. Cognitive impairment and dementia

### Takeaway
LASI-DAD estimates dementia prevalence at **7.4%** (95% CI 6.4–8.5) among Indians 60+, or about **8.8 million** people. That is much higher than every earlier estimate: ARDSI 2010 (3.7 million), ARDSI 2020 (5.29 million), GBD 2019 India (3.69 million, all ages) and meta-analyses (20 or 33.7 per 1,000). ### Inferences
- The gap between ~2–3.4% (older community studies and meta-analyses) and 7.4% (LASI-DAD) is probably methodological. LASI-DAD used a culturally adapted, nationally representative clinical-consensus design that includes CDR 0.5 cases. Older studies mostly used DSM-based diagnosis in single sites. Rodriguez 2008 shows the same population can yield 0.3–0.9% (DSM-IV) or 8.2–8.7% (10/66 algorithm).
- The count conflicts (3.69M, 3.7M, 3.84M, 5.29M, 8.8M) mix all-ages GBD counts with 60+ (or 65+) estimates and different reference years. Comparisons must state the age band and year.
- Dementia is concentrated among women (9.03%), rural residents (8.35%), people with no formal education (10.29%) and people 85+ (25.41%). These groups overlap with low literacy and low near vision, which compounds difficulty at S2/S3 (forms, OTPs), S5 (history-taking, consent), S8 (adherence with polypharmacy) and S9 (remembering follow-ups). Many will need a proxy or caregiver.

### Gaps
- The LASI-DAD reference year for the 8.8 million count is ambiguous in the fetched text: Table 2 is described as using 2016 population figures, but the Discussion refers to 2019. Check the Table 2 footnote before quoting a year.
- The ARDSI 2010 and 2020 primary PDFs were not opened (ardsi.org link cited by STRiDE: https://ardsi.org/pdf/annual%20report.pdf). The age band (60+ vs 65+) and the 2010 vs 2020 attribution of "5.29 million" remain unresolved.
- No separate prevalence of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) for India 60+ was collected in this search.

## 6. Extras: ADL/IADL functional limitation and depression, 60+

LASI Wave 1 (2017-18): **24%** of 60+ have at least one ADL limitation and **48%** at least one IADL limitation. ## Gaps and contradictions

**Contradictions recorded side by side (not resolved)**
1. **Any chronic condition, 60+ (LASI 2017-18):** 75% (minister's launch statement, news) vs 55% (Deccan Herald). Neither appears in the LASI Executive Summary text.
2. **Multimorbidity, 60+ (same LASI data):** 23% (LASI-ES p.16) vs 24.1% (Chauhan, BMJ Open 2022) vs 27% / ~35 million (Deccan Herald) vs 32.1% (Patel, BMC Geriatrics 2023, 11 conditions).
3. **Polypharmacy:** 49% pooled, mostly hospital (Bhagavathula 2021; 5–9 medicines) vs 33.7% urban community (Das 2025; ≥5 daily for ≥4 weeks). Within the meta-analysis, the North-east estimate is 72% in the Results and 65% in the Abstract.
4. **NBVIS VI (50+):** Table 3 gives 26.68% and Table 5 gives 28.68% for the same ≥50 group in the same paper. Avoidable VI is 97.4% (PLOS paper) vs 96.2% (secondary summaries). Whole-population blindness of 0.36% (Table 5, all ages) circulates alongside 1.99% (50+); these are different denominators.
5. **Vision, LASI vs NBVIS:** blindness 3.8% (LASI, 60+, measured with available correction, 2017-18) vs 1.58% (60–69), 4.11% (70–79) and 11.62% (≥80) (NBVIS, presenting VA, 2015–19). The methods and thresholds differ, and LASI-ES does not print its thresholds.
6. **Hearing, 60+:** 10% / 9.6 self-reported problems (LASI) vs 21% difficulty (BKPAI, seven states) vs "two per cent" hearing disability (Down To Earth reporting LASI) vs 0.3% hearing disability, all ages (NSS 2018) vs a large majority with PTA >25 dB in LASI-DAD audiometry (counts only). Different constructs: problem, difficulty, disability and measured loss.
7. **Hearing aid use:** "around 1%" of all 60+ (LASI-ES) vs 7.8% of those with hearing difficulty (Roy & Das 2026, LASI) vs 5% (BKPAI). Different denominators.
8. **Dementia, prevalence:** 7.4% (LASI-DAD, 60+) vs 20 per 1,000 (Choudhary 2021) vs 33.7 per 1,000 (Dhiman 2021) vs 3.4%–4.4% (meta-analyses cited by STRiDE) vs single-site studies from 0.3% (DSM-IV) to 14.9%.
9. **Dementia, counts:** 8.8 million (LASI-DAD, 60+) vs 3.7 million (ARDSI 2010; >60 or >65 depending on source) vs 5.29 million (ARDSI 2020 per PARI; attributed to ARDSI 2010 by STRiDE) vs 3.69 million (GBD 2019 India, all ages, Lancet GH 2021) vs 3,843,118 (GBD 2019 forecasting paper as reported by news, all ages).
10. **The same individual dementia studies printed differently:** Banerjee 2017 Kolkata is 1.3 (LASI-DAD Table 3) vs 1.53% ≥60 (STRiDE). Vas 2001 is 2.3 vs 2.44% (≥65).
11. **ADL/IADL:** IADL 48% / 48.0 (LASI-ES) vs 48.4% (a 2021 re-analysis, snippet only).
