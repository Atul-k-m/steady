# Clinician voice: what Indian clinicians say goes wrong when older patients (60+) use healthcare

## Key Question 1: Which problems do clinicians raise most often, and in their own words?

### Takeaway
The problems Indian clinicians raise most often, in their own words, are: (1) polypharmacy and prescriptions from several doctors that nobody reconciles; (2) a health system that treats older people "the way it treated younger people", with too few trained geriatricians; (3) older patients who cannot physically or cognitively get through hospitals, who come alone, or who depend on an escort; (4) hearing loss, mood disorders and dementia that are normalised or missed; and (5) family and caregiver gaps. Very few verifiable clinician quotes cover scattered records, brand/generic confusion, ABHA/digital IDs or insurance paperwork.

### Cited Findings

#### Method and verification key (read first)
- **Verification "RAW"**: I downloaded the page HTML or PDF, extracted the text, and copied the quote from that text. **"WF×2"**: the raw page was bot-blocked (ThePrint), so the quote was checked through two separate WebFetch extractions of the live page. Both matched.
- Wording, spelling, grammar and punctuation are reproduced as on the source page; the only normalisation is that curly apostrophes (’) are rendered as straight apostrophes ('). `[...]` marks my omissions. `[square-bracket words]` inside quotes are the source's own insertions unless marked "[my note]".
- **Quality tags**: peer-reviewed / report / news / talk. **Stages**: S1 Find doctor · S2 Book · S3 Register · S4 Wait/navigate · S5 Consultation · S6 Tests · S7 Reports · S8 Medicines · S9 Follow-ups · S10 Payments/insurance · S11 Digital IDs/teleconsult · S12 Emergencies · S0 Other.
- **Numbers**: every figure a clinician cites is marked **[clinician-cited, not verified]** unless it is traced to a primary source.

### Inferences
- The single most consistent clinician complaint is **medicines prescribed in silos**, with no one reconciling them. Geriatricians (Q1, Q6, Q7), hospital specialists (Q16), ophthalmologists (Q25) and rural providers (Q19, Q20) arrive at it from different angles. Geriatricians frame it as interaction risk. Rural providers frame it as low-quality, one-size prescribing and patients habituated to medicines.
- Clinicians describe the hospital visit itself as the barrier as often as the disease: getting there, needing an escort, finding the way, waiting, and being referred between departments (Q12–Q14, Q21). This is S4 "navigation" more than S5 "consultation".
- Sensory and cognitive problems are described less as **hidden by patients** and more as **normalised by everyone**. Patients, families and doctors write them off as ageing (Q3 paraphrase, Q9), and dementia is "the end of the clinical road" for many physicians (Q6).
- Records and report-handling (S7) appear mainly as a coordination problem ("Not one place", Q16), not as an explicit complaint about patients arriving without files. That specific complaint may be common in practice, but I found no on-record clinician saying it.

### Gaps
- No verifiable named-clinician quote on patients arriving without previous reports or with scattered paper files, families not knowing the medication list, brand vs generic confusion, inability to read labels, ABHA or insurance paperwork, or elderly people being unable to stand in OPD queues. The queue/2 a.m. token anecdote I found is from a HelpAge India writer who is not a clinician (not included).
- Several quotes come from anonymised qualitative participants (Q12–Q20, Q24–Q26). They are "clearly identified" by role but not by name.
- ThePrint (Q1–Q2) could not be fetched raw because of bot-blocking. Those quotes rest on two consistent WebFetch extractions.

## Key Question 2: Which specialties emphasise which problems?

### Takeaway
Geriatricians emphasise workforce shortage, polypharmacy and a system designed for younger adults. ENT specialists emphasise normalised hearing loss and hearing-aid stigma and cost. The ophthalmologists sampled emphasise consultation time and medicine access and technique, not cataract delay. Hospital specialists emphasise getting to and around the hospital, follow-up and coordination. Psychiatrists, neurologists and GPs emphasise teleconsultation limits and caregiver absence. Palliative clinicians and nurses emphasise family costs and neglect.

### Cited Findings
- **Geriatric medicine (Dey, Chakravarty, Chatterjee, Kannan)**: shortage of trained geriatricians and training ([Medical Dialogues](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/latest-news/geriatric-care-is-no-longer-optional-it-is-a-national-imperative-for-indias-ageing-population-say-experts-163882); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/geriatric-medicine-india-new-priority-elderly-forgotten-youngistan/2592831)). Older people treated "the way it treated younger people" ([IndiaSpend](https://www.isignal.in/indiaspend-interviews/india-must-ensure-elders-security-sustenance-access-to-health-776940)). Polypharmacy and interactions ([Interview World](https://theinterview.world/building-a-5-ms-framework-in-modern-geriatric-care/); [Healthcare Executive](https://www.healthcareexecutive.in/blog/holistic-view-of-senior-care)). Overlooked mood and dementia, and the caregiver gap ([Interview World](https://theinterview.world/building-a-5-ms-framework-in-modern-geriatric-care/)). Limited consultation time and hospital design ([Healthcare Executive](https://www.healthcareexecutive.in/blog/holistic-view-of-senior-care)).
- **ENT/otology (Kanotra, K. Nath, N. Nath, B.P. Dey)**: hearing loss "dismissed as an inevitable part of ageing" and first noticed by family; misheard instructions about medicines; stigma ("look old or disabled") ([Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/hearing-loss-in-the-elderly/)). Hearing aids seen as "odd"; cost of discreet aids; preference for medicine over aids ([Barak Bulletin](https://barakbulletin.com/?p=52548)). No audiologist quotes found.
- **Ophthalmology (glaucoma ophthalmologists, LVPEI-linked study; Marmamula, LVPEI)**: "too many patients" and too little time; prescriptions "not available in their hometown"; patients unable to instil drops after years of care; phone consults as a workaround ([Bott et al., IJO 2025](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/36310/)). Vision loss common and linked to depression and cognitive decline ([IJO letter 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9907249/)). **No ophthalmologist quote found on cataract-surgery delay.**
- **Hospital specialists and administrators (cardiology/neurology/orthopaedics, Hyderabad private hospital)**: travel and escort; the route from vehicle to consulting room; minor problems needing a hospital trip, appointment and wait; missed follow-up; several doctors with overlapping medicines; no single information point; split views on specialists vs MBBS/family medicine ([Int J Geriatr Gerontol](https://www.geriatricsandgerontology.com/article/view/experiences-of-healthcare-providers-in-care-delivery-to-older-persons)).
- **Psychiatry, neurology and GPs (dementia care, south India)**: phone/video assessment failing, proxy consults, unavailable memantine and psychotropics, families afraid of admission, GPs "prescribing the same meds" ([Frontiers in Psychiatry 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7854902/)).
- **Orthopaedics (rural UP)**: delayed care through home remedies and masseuses ("sutwana"); Ayushman cards as a precondition for knee replacement ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/geriatric-medicine-india-new-priority-elderly-forgotten-youngistan/2592831)).
- **Resident doctor (dermatology, public hospital)**: older patients coming alone, confused navigation, being passed between departments, deafness blocking history-taking, ageist remarks by doctors ([IJME 2015](https://ijme.in/articles/are-we-treating-our-geriatric-population-ethically-views-of-a-resident-doctor/?galley=print)).
- **Rural frontline providers (RMPs, PHC doctor, private provider, Telangana)**: government hospitals giving "the same medicines [...] to everybody"; habituation to medicines; family dynamics ([BMC Geriatrics 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5517927/)).
- **Palliative medicine and nursing (Damani, Pallium India, Kerala nurses)**: indirect costs and an insurance gap; OPD substituting for home care; caregivers' poverty; nurses confronting neglect of parents ([Newslaundry/IndiaSpend](https://www.newslaundry.com/2026/03/09/elder-care-was-meant-to-reach-homes-in-most-of-india-it-hasnt); [Int J Equity Health 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11077822/)).
- **ASHAs (arthritis education, Udupi)**: community members wanting medicines or a hospital visit rather than education; distrust because of short training ([JCDR 2017](https://www.jcdr.net/ReadXMLFile.aspx?id=9530)).
- **Neuropsychiatry institute leadership (IHBAS)**: nuclear families and social isolation ([Medical Dialogues](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/latest-news/geriatric-care-is-no-longer-optional-it-is-a-national-imperative-for-indias-ageing-population-say-experts-163882)).

### Inferences
- Specialists tend to name the problem their own clinic sees at the point of failure. ENT sees normalisation and stigma. Glaucoma clinics see medicine access and technique. Geriatricians see the accumulated prescriptions. Hospital specialists see the logistics of getting there. No specialty "owns" the whole journey, which matches Kannan's call to "rethink the elderly patient's entire journey" ([Healthcare Executive](https://www.healthcareexecutive.in/blog/holistic-view-of-senior-care)).
- Geriatricians are the most quoted group in Indian media. Frontline voices (ASHAs, nurses, pharmacists) appear almost only inside qualitative studies, and rarely about older patients specifically.

### Gaps

## Key Question 3: Do clinicians contradict each other or contradict survey data? (Gaps and contradictions)

### Takeaway
Clinicians mostly agree with survey data on polypharmacy and self-medication. They disagree with each other on two points: whether older people need geriatric specialists or "a plain MBBS doctor", and whether teleconsultation works for older patients. Survey data also point to a barrier that the clinicians I found rarely voice: needing a family member to accompany the patient, which was the top reason for late cataract presentation in one study. The brief's assumption that older patients "hide" hearing problems is only partly supported. ENT specialists describe hearing loss among older people as normalised and stigmatised, and say active concealment is more typical of younger patients.

### Cited Findings
- **Consistent with survey data, on polypharmacy and self-medication**:
  - The survey: a cross-sectional door-to-door study of 600 older adults in six cities (Scientific Reports, Feb 2025) found polypharmacy in 33.7% and self-medication in 19.7%, and 35.8% of the medicines flagged as potentially inappropriate involved duplicate prescriptions. Polypharmacy risk was about 3 times higher for people moving between healthcare providers, and self-medication about 4.5 times higher for those living alone ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/wrong-prescriptions-self-medication-put-indias-ageing-population-at-risk-study/), linking to [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84627-2)).
  - The clinician side: this matches "They see several doctors and there is an overlap of diseases and medications" (Q16), the "Each speciality has its focus" chain (Q7), and "They come to us to take medicines for even a very small pain" (Q20).
- **Clinicians extrapolate where Indian data are missing**: Kannan uses a US figure (one in six ER visits medication-related) and says "We don't have these statistics for India" ([Healthcare Executive](https://www.healthcareexecutive.in/blog/holistic-view-of-senior-care)).
- **Clinician vs clinician, on specialists or generalists**:
  - For specialists: Chakravarty ("trained doctors who understand how a cocktail of drugs could adversely affect a patient", [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/geriatric-medicine-india-new-priority-elderly-forgotten-youngistan/2592831)) and Dey ("need for geriatricians who are trained to see the person in the context of age", [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/elderly-lonely-44173)).
  - Against: a Hyderabad specialist says "I don't want specialists [...] Geriatric care should be given by everybody and also by a plain MBBS doctor", and another says "family medicine people should take care of geriatric people" ([Int J Geriatr Gerontol](https://www.geriatricsandgerontology.com/article/view/experiences-of-healthcare-providers-in-care-delivery-to-older-persons)).
- **Clinician vs clinician, on teleconsultation**:
  - Positive: Kannan says "Teleconsultations have transformed care delivery" ([Healthcare Executive](https://www.healthcareexecutive.in/blog/holistic-view-of-senior-care)); one dementia physician found rural clients "quite tech-savvy" ([PMC7854902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7854902/)); a glaucoma ophthalmologist says "Many things I manage on the phone" ([Bott et al.](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/36310/)).
  - Negative: other physicians in the same dementia study say "very difficult to apply scales over phone/video", "Most virtual consults were on a proxy", and "I often don't feel we are doing enough" ([PMC7854902](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7854902/)).
  - The conference write-up's own (unattributed) summary says "many seniors struggling to adopt technology" ([Medical Dialogues](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/latest-news/geriatric-care-is-no-longer-optional-it-is-a-national-imperative-for-indias-ageing-population-say-experts-163882)).
- **Clinician vs clinician, on community and ASHA-led care**: Dey (2021) calls the ASHA-led geriatric vertical "a very idealistic programme of an ASHA worker with a 120-page manual" ([IndiaSpend](https://www.isignal.in/indiaspend-interviews/india-must-ensure-elders-security-sustenance-access-to-health-776940)). Chatterjee (2026) argues that "Developing certified community-based caregivers is therefore critical" ([Interview World](https://theinterview.world/building-a-5-ms-framework-in-modern-geriatric-care/)). This is a difference in which cadre should carry the work, not a flat contradiction. The ASHAs' own account of credibility problems after "only 21 days training" ([JCDR](https://www.jcdr.net/ReadXMLFile.aspx?id=9530)) is consistent with Dey's scepticism.
- **Survey vs clinician emphasis, on escort and accompaniment**:
  - The survey: among 206 patients with advanced cataract at a North Karnataka tertiary centre, "The most frequent reason for late presentation to the hospital was lack of an accompanying family member/caretaker (24.27%), followed by good vision in the other eye (22.33%)". Other reasons were fear of surgery (16.02%) and possible loss of income (12.62%), and 76.69% of patients were illiterate ([IJCMPH 2025](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/14221)).
  - The clinician side: no ophthalmologist quote I found raises the escort barrier. Non-eye clinicians do: "somebody to accompany them makes a lot of difference" ([Int J Geriatr Gerontol](https://www.geriatricsandgerontology.com/article/view/experiences-of-healthcare-providers-in-care-delivery-to-older-persons)); "most elderly patients came alone" ([IJME](https://ijme.in/articles/are-we-treating-our-geriatric-population-ethically-views-of-a-resident-doctor/?galley=print)).
- **The "hiding hearing loss" premise is only partly supported**: Dr Kripamoy Nath attributes active hiding mainly to the young ("Young patients definitely try to hide it") ([Barak Bulletin](https://barakbulletin.com/?p=52548)). Dr Kanotra frames it among older people as normalisation, noticed first by family ([Daily Excelsior](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/hearing-loss-in-the-elderly/)). The resident doctor in Q21 describes deafness openly blocking the consultation, not concealed ([IJME](https://ijme.in/articles/are-we-treating-our-geriatric-population-ethically-views-of-a-resident-doctor/?galley=print)).
- **Families: three different pictures**:
  - Older patients who "came alone" because children did not accompany them ([IJME](https://ijme.in/articles/are-we-treating-our-geriatric-population-ethically-views-of-a-resident-doctor/?galley=print)).
  - Older people who "don't want to disturb their family members" ([Int J Geriatr Gerontol](https://www.geriatricsandgerontology.com/article/view/experiences-of-healthcare-providers-in-care-delivery-to-older-persons)).
  - Families who object to neighbours helping an older relative (a rural Telangana RMP: "their family members will question us “why are you helping them? Aren't we there?”") ([BMC Geriatrics 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5517927/)).
- **Less medicine or specialised drugs**: Dey's "give less medicine for maximum advantage" ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/environment/elderly-lonely-44173)) sits alongside Chakravarty's "they need specialised drugs" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/geriatric-medicine-india-new-priority-elderly-forgotten-youngistan/2592831)). Both argue for appropriate rather than more prescribing, so this is a nuance rather than a contradiction.

### Inferences
- The disagreement on specialists vs generalists matters for how claims are framed. Geriatricians' calls for more geriatricians come from a specialty with an interest in expansion. Hospital specialists and rural providers point to generalists or family medicine. Neither side offers data on outcomes.
- Clinicians' optimism about teleconsultation tends to assume a caregiver or proxy is present (Kannan's "caregiver brings the reports"; the dementia "proxy" consults). Older people living alone may be the group least served by it. This is consistent with the survey finding that living alone raises self-medication risk ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/wrong-prescriptions-self-medication-put-indias-ageing-population-at-risk-study/)).
- The escort barrier is visible in patient-side data and in generalist testimony, but under-voiced by the specialists who perform the procedures. It may be invisible from inside the clinic, because patients without escorts never arrive.

### Gaps

- **Themes with no verified clinician quotes**: patients arriving without records or with paper bundles; families not knowing the medication list; brand vs generic confusion; illegible prescriptions or labels; ABHA/digital health ID problems; insurance paperwork other than palliative coverage (Q22); children living abroad. Dhamija's "nuclear families" (Q28) is non-specific. The conference's migration remark came from Anil Rajput, a non-clinician, and is not included.
