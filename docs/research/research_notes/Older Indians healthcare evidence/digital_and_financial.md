How to read these notes
- Every row gives: the value exactly as printed, the population or denominator, the data-collection year (and publication year), the source, a quality tag (govt survey / peer-reviewed / report / news) and care-journey stage tags (S0-S12).
- No numbers below were rounded, combined across sources or computed by me. Where a source's age band is not 60+, the band is given exactly as printed.
- **Verification column:** "Text" means I read the number in the source document itself (PDF text extraction or HTML). "Snippet" means the number comes only from a search-engine summary of that URL, because the page was blocked or unreachable from my environment. Treat "Snippet" rows as leads to re-check before they go on a fact sheet.

## 1. Mobile phone and smartphone ownership among 60+

### Takeaway
No nationally representative **government** survey I could reach prints *individual* mobile or smartphone ownership for the 60+ band. LASI Wave 1 (2017-18) reports only *household* phone ownership, in households with at least one member aged 45+. A non-government analysis of NSS CMS:Telecom 2025 unit data puts smartphone access at 44.7% (ages 60-69) and 42% (70+). HelpAge India's 2024 urban survey finds 39% of older people have access to a smartphone and 59% have no digital device at all.

### Inferences
- The only primary government dataset in reach that tabulates phones near this age group (LASI) measures **households with a 45+ member**. A phone in the household does not mean the older person owns or uses it. The HelpAge figures (59% with no device) and the CMS:T-based figures (about 42-45% smartphone access at 60+) suggest the gap between household and individual access is large. This is an inference from different sources, not a measured gap.
- Access falls steeply with age (HelpAge: 26% of 80+ have any device) and is lower for women (33% vs 48%). Both matter for S1 (finding a doctor) and S2 (booking) wherever these steps assume the patient has a phone.

### Gaps
- LASI Wave 1: no individual-level phone ownership or use indicator for 60+ in the India Report or either factsheet (I searched the text for "phone", "mobile" and "internet"). **NOT FOUND.**
- CMS:Telecom 2025 full report: the press note gives only the 15-29 and 15+ bands. Whether the full MoSPI report tabulates 60+ was not checked; the full report PDF was not retrieved.
- IAMAI-Kantar *Internet in India 2024*: has no older age band (see Section 2). Pew and CMIE age-band data for India: **NOT FOUND** (not located in the searches run).

## 2. Internet use among 60+

### Takeaway
The best official 60+ figure is from NSS 75th round (2017-18). It finds **3.2%** of persons aged 60 years and above used the internet in the last 30 days (rural 0.8%, urban 8.9%), and **3.9%** were able to use the internet. Later non-official figures are much higher: "13 percent… have ever used the internet" (NITI Aayog 2024, as cited by ORF) and "around two in five" aged 60+ can use it (CMS:T 2025 analysis). These come from different years, definitions and denominators and should not be compared directly.

### Inferences
- In 2017-18 internet use at 60+ was almost absent in rural India (0.8% used it in the last 30 days) and very low among older women (1.5%). Any care step that runs only online (S2 booking, S7 reports, S11 teleconsult or ABHA) would have excluded most older adults at that time.
- The later figures suggest rapid growth, but they cannot be lined up as a trend with the NSS figure. "Ever used" (13%), "can use" (two in five) and "used in last 30 days" (3.2%) are different constructs, from different producers, collected seven to eight years apart.

### Gaps
- LASI Wave 1: no internet-use indicator for 60+ in the India Report or factsheets. **NOT FOUND.**
- NSS 78th MIS (2020-21): no individual internet-use-by-age statement found in the summary chapter. **NOT FOUND** for 60+.
- IAMAI-Kantar 2023 report: not checked. The 2024 report has no older age band. **NOT FOUND** for 60+.
- NSS 79th CAMS (2022-23) and the full CMS:T 2025 report: not retrieved, so it is unknown whether either prints a 60+ band.
- The original NITI Aayog 2024 paper was not opened, so the data source behind its "13 percent" figure is unverified.

## 3. Digital literacy and ICT skills among 60+

### Takeaway
NSS 75th round (2017-18) **does isolate "60 years and above"**: **3.5%** were able to operate a computer (rural 0.9%, urban 9.5%; men 5.3%, women 1.7%). NSS 78th MIS (2020-21) **does not**: its ICT-skill tables use only "15-24 years", "15-29 years" and "15 years and above". HelpAge 2024 finds that only "1 in every five elderly" can use digital devices comfortably and 12% used them for bill payment or internet banking. A CMS:T 2025 analysis reports 53.1% of 60+ say they can do online banking, but its denominator is unclear.

- The NSS 78th MIS was designed around SDG indicator 4.4.1, which is youth- and adult-focused (15-24, 15+). That is why it cannot answer the 60+ question.

### Gaps
- NSS 78th MIS: 60+ ICT skills are **NOT FOUND** in printed tables. The oldest band is "15 years and above". Unit-level data on microdata.gov.in could produce a 60+ estimate, but that would be new computation, outside this brief.
- No official 60+ figure was found for "can send a message", "can make a digital payment" or "can use a search engine". **NOT FOUND** in NSS 585, MIS 589 or the CMS:T press note.
- Agewell figures are snippet-level only and nearly a decade old. No newer Agewell digital survey was located.

## 4. Health insurance coverage among 60+, incl. Ayushman Vay Vandana (PM-JAY 70+)

### Takeaway
LASI Wave 1 (2017-18) prints **18.2%** health-insurance coverage among persons aged 60 and above (men 19.7%, women 16.9%). Only 5.2% of 60+ financed their last hospitalisation through insurance or employer reimbursement. The 2024 Vay Vandana expansion grew from "more than 10 lakh" enrolled (Nov 2024) to "more than 1.36 crore senior citizens" holding cards "as on 21.09.26" (PIB, Sep 2026), against an eligible population the government puts at "approximately 6 crore senior citizens across 4.5 crore families". No survey-based post-2024 60+ coverage figure exists yet; LASI Wave 2 results were not found.

## 5. Out-of-pocket (OOP) health spending: national share and elderly-specific costs

### Takeaway
National Health Accounts put OOPE at **39.4% of Total Health Expenditure in 2021-22**, down from 62.6% in 2014-15. For older adults, LASI Wave 1 (2017-18) prints a mean expenditure on the last hospitalisation of **INR 22797** for persons aged 60+ (public INR 8028, private INR 31930). It also finds **34.9%** of 45+ households incurred catastrophic health expenditure at a 10% threshold. Official NSS reports do **not** print hospitalisation cost by age. An NSS-based peer-reviewed study reports average elderly inpatient OOP of **INR 23,234** and CHE incidence of **46.5%** at a 10% threshold.

### Inferences
- National OOPE has fallen as a share of THE, but older adults still face high household-level financial risk. In 2017-18, 34.9% of 45+ households (LASI) and 46.5% of older adults who needed care (NSS-based study) crossed a 10% CHE threshold. These two figures use different units and definitions (household vs individual; consumption vs capacity-to-pay) and must not be compared directly.
- In LASI, a private hospitalisation for a 60+ person cost almost four times a public one (INR 31930 vs INR 8028, as printed). 59.1% of last hospitalisations were in private facilities. This makes S10 (payment and insurance) the decisive step for inpatient care. This is an interpretation of the printed values; I computed no ratio beyond reading them side by side.
- LASI's mean cost for 60+ is *lower* than for 45-59 (22797 vs 50493). One possible reason is that older adults use cheaper or public care more often, or forgo care. That is an inference only; the factsheet gives no explanation.
