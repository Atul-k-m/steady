## Q1. How many Indians are aged 60+, now and projected to 2036 and 2050 (count and share)?

### Takeaway
No single source covers "now" through 2050. The most recent official share is SRS 2024: **9.7%** of the population aged 60+. The most recent official count is a TG projection: **1,37,897 thousand in 2021 (10.1%)**, rising to **2,28,059 thousand in 2036 (15.0%)**. UNFPA's India Ageing Report 2023, using UN WPP 2022, gives **149 million (10.5%) on 1 July 2022** and **347 million (20.8%) in 2050**. The 2050 figure exists only in the UN-based series, because the TG projections stop at 2036.

### Inferences
- "Now" depends on the source. The latest official count (as opposed to a share) is a TG projection: 1,63,148 thousand for 1 March 2026 (row 1.6). The latest measured share is SRS 2024 at 9.7% (row 1.13). These two series disagree (see Q6), so the fact sheet should present them side by side, not merge them.
- About 71% of older persons lived in rural areas at Census 2011. That points to distance and transport as part of the healthcare-access context (S1 Find doctor, S4 Wait/navigate). This is an inference from the residence share, not a measured access finding.

### Gaps
- No Indian government count of the 60+ population after 2021 that is a measurement rather than a projection. Census 2021 has not been published, and SRS gives shares only.
- I did not access UN WPP 2024 directly, so the latest UN-based count for 2025 or 2026 is NOT FOUND in these notes.
- I did not open Census 2011 primary tables (C-series) on censusindia.gov.in. Census 2011 values above are as printed in MoSPI-EII, and as quoted by UNFPA-IAR.

## Q2. How many Indians are aged 70+ and 80+, now and projected?

### Takeaway
No source accessed prints a combined **70+** total for India. Sources print 70-74, 75-79 and 80+ separately, and these must not be added up (per the brief). For **80+**, the TG projects **6,464 thousand (2011) → 14,611 thousand (2021) → 27,536 thousand (2036)**, with women outnumbering men in every year. UNFPA projects that the 80+ population will grow **279% between 2022 and 2050** but prints no 2050 count.

## Q3. What share of people aged 60+ live alone, or with their spouse only?

### Takeaway
Living alone is about **5.7%** in LASI 2017-18 and **4.2%** in NSS 2017-18. In both surveys it is much higher for women (LASI: 9.0%, or 8.5% in the India Report; NSS: 6.6%) than for men (about 1.6-2.6%). Living with spouse only is about **14.1%** of persons in NSS (men 17.9%, women 10.4%). UNFPA's analysis of LASI puts it at **19.4% of men and 12.0% of women**. LASI's own published tables do **not** isolate "spouse only". Their category is "Living with spouse and/or others" (20.3%).

### Inferences
- In both 2017-18 surveys, older women are far more likely than older men to live alone (LASI 9.0% vs 2.6%; NSS 6.6% vs 1.6%). Older women living with children may depend on those children to arrange and pay for care (S10). This is an inference; neither survey measures companion availability at visits.
- Couples living alone ("spouse only", roughly one in five men) depend on an ageing spouse as the main helper. LASI-IR notes that "the elderly living alone or living only with a spouse is an increasing trend, particularly in urban areas" ([LASI-IR, Introduction](https://iipsindia.ac.in/sites/default/files/LASI_India_Report_2020_compressed.pdf)).
- Institutional living barely registers. NSS records 0 old-age-home inmates because it samples households, so it cannot measure people in institutions.

- UNFPA's Figure 2.15 values are read from the chart's text layer. The India values (2.5 / 19.4 / 8.6 / 12.0) match the report's text for living alone, but the spouse-exclusive values should be checked visually in the PDF.
